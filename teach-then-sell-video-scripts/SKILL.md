# Teach-then-sell video scripts

For expert, founder or salesperson-led videos on Meta, LinkedIn or YouTube that connect with buyers and still sell, without sounding like a pitch.

## Inputs

- Company and product: `<fill in>`
- Topic and buyer (job title): `<fill in>`
- Presenter: `<fill in>`
- Buyer research and verbatim quote library: `<fill in>`
- Outside stats, each with source and year: `<fill in>`
- Product capability baseline and approved customer proof: `<fill in>`

If any input is missing, ask before drafting. Never invent a stat, quote or capability.

## Hooks

Write five hook options per concept, each as spoken line / on-screen text / visual action in the first frame. Types that work for B2B:

- a specific stat from the research;
- a verbatim buyer quote;
- a bold statement the buyer will recognise as true;
- a question about their own week;
- a named moment from the buyer's world (a request from their boss, a renewal, a deadline).

Make each one specific enough to picture and check. Every script also gets a second hook option written to lead straight into the script's next line, so both can be filmed and tested on the same body.

## Beat structure (60 to 90 seconds)

| Beat | Job |
|---|---|
| 0 to 3s hook | Stat, quote or bold line, with a visual action. |
| Pattern | What's really happening, in the buyer's words. |
| Opinion beat | A short reaction from the buyer's point of view, in regular language, with a gesture cue (*sighs*, *points at camera*). Write it from the side of the person who buys, not the end user. |
| How most solutions work | Teach the mechanism in two or three sentences. |
| Where it breaks | The specific cases it can't handle, and the consequence. |
| What to check | Two to four questions the viewer can ask any vendor or their own team. |
| Product as proof | One line using a turn ("That's why we built [product]..." or "That's what [customer] did with [product]..."), plus approved proof or three seconds of the product doing the task. |
| Close | A low-pressure question the viewer can answer about their own world, said and shown at the same time. |

Format each script as a table: Time / What you say / On screen.

## Cutdowns and format

- A 30-second cut of every script from the same beats: hook, problem, where it breaks, what to check, question. The product line can be left out of the cold-audience cut and kept for retargeting.
- Shoot 9:16 first, burn in full captions, keep text out of platform safe zones, and get the brand on screen early even if it's only said near the end.

## Test plan

Test the hook first: three hooks on the same body. Judge on hook rate (3-second views divided by impressions; 30% or more is strong) and hold rate (15-second views divided by 3-second views), not leads, at B2B budgets. Run at least seven days per round. Keep the winning hook and write two new ones in its style.

## Claims

- Every stat shows its source on screen.
- Buyer quotes stay anonymous and need sign-off.
- Customer stats need that customer's approval, and the same customer proof shouldn't carry every ad in a set.
- Never use a figure flagged as unverified.

## Writing rules

Plain spoken language. No "it's not X, it's Y", no em dashes, no fake questions, no pun or clever flip, no build-up before the point. Read every line aloud.
