# Buyer Research to Ads

Make advertising sound like it understands the buyer's actual work. Carry concrete language from research into a clear commercial message, then show what the product changes. Keep source evidence, creative interpretation and product claims distinct.

This skill develops research, messaging and creative direction. Produce artwork when requested and tools are available. It does not itself authorize publishing, paid-media changes, outreach or writes to a customer's systems.

“Product” means the company's offering: software, physical goods or a service. For consumer products and services, use the relevant buying, use or delivery experience instead of forcing a software workflow or product UI.

## Establish the assignment

Use supplied company context before asking for information it already contains. Identify the product, buyers, use cases, verified capabilities, campaign objective, buying stage, brand voice, visual references, placements and deliverables.

Resolve gaps that materially change the work. Otherwise proceed with a stated, limited assumption. Do not turn a request for angles into an unsolicited production batch. When scope is open, choose a manageable set of distinct directions and explain coverage.

Read [Evidence to angles](references/evidence-to-angles.md) for source analysis, excerpt selection and use-case mapping.

## Research before writing

Read the supplied sources, including relevant linked use-case pages. Comprehensive requests require coverage of pains, aspirations, reactions, objections and buyer roles. Follow continuations when content is truncated; do not call a partial read complete. Prefer connected tools for sources they support. If access fails, use accessible files or pasted content and state the coverage limit.

Build a source-linked evidence bank. Preserve exact excerpts separately from paraphrases and researcher synthesis. Capture what the person was discussing, not just the most enthusiastic sentence. Deduplicate repeated calls from the same account before describing breadth.

Classify evidence before using it:

- **Current capability:** supported by product documentation, an explicit supplied capability or a clearly scoped demonstration/deployment.
- **Pain or aspiration:** evidence of demand, not proof the product solves it.
- **Observed reaction or outcome:** retain the feature, context and measurement limits.
- **Objection or unresolved question:** useful for angles; not permission to invent the missing answer.

A general-purpose product does not automatically support every integration or action a buyer requests. When a desired capability is unverified, develop the pain or a supported adjacent workflow, or label the direction exploratory. Do not turn it into an unqualified product claim.

## Find angles, not just quotations

For each requested theme or use case, identify:

1. **Who feels it:** role, responsibility and buying context.
2. **The moment:** a recognizable trigger, request, deadline or handoff.
3. **The friction:** what repeats, stalls, gets re-entered, is missed or requires chasing.
4. **The consequence:** time, confidence, experience, capacity, visibility or business risk.
5. **The desired change:** what would feel meaningfully better.
6. **The product's job:** the supported action connecting that change to the offering.

Develop different routes where evidence supports them: pain recognition, a completed workflow, a business outcome, a larger functional ambition, an objection answered or proof. Change the underlying tension or promise, not merely synonyms in a headline.

For several use cases, give each its own triggers, artifacts, owners and outcomes. Do not paste a generic platform slogan across every section. A platform story can sit above use cases, but should name the audience and the new scope of work it enables.

## Write the ad

Read [Writing and examples](references/writing-and-examples.md) for the copy standard and fictional worked examples.

- Lead with a concrete pain, desired progress or specific opportunity. The buyer should recognize the situation without reading the research.
- Make the audience and category evident across the headline, supporting line and image. Every headline need not carry all three when the full asset is clear.
- Use short, direct language in the buyer's register. Preserve specificity; remove transcript filler.
- Make the supporting line explain what the product actually does. Connect action to benefit rather than listing features or repeating the headline.
- Match the scope: an answer, a document, a routed case, an approved change, a completed transaction and a platform spanning several jobs are different promises.
- Keep controls and architecture secondary unless they are the buyer's primary concern or the angle being tested.
- Ground visionary copy in the function, business change and product's credible role. Competitive urgency needs evidence; do not invent adoption pressure or falling-behind claims.
- Offer alternatives that test a meaningful difference. Follow the requested count; three routes can be useful for exploration but are not mandatory.

Do not present an adapted line as an exact quote or as an approved endorsement. Keep identifiable internal attribution in the research record as appropriate. Unattributed brand-copy drafts can proceed without treating testimonial clearance as a blocker.

## Make the work visible when useful

For briefs or artwork, read [Creative and review](references/creative-and-review.md). Use the current company's brand and approved product design, not a prior client's logo, palette, typefaces, UI or imagery.

Show a concrete experience when it clarifies value: request, action and a completed outcome or explicit handoff. Include human approval before actions that depend on it. Creating a case does not resolve the underlying issue; preparing a draft does not send it; queuing a payment does not mean money arrived.

Photography, product UI, typography, diagrams and proof-led layouts are choices, not a compulsory template. Match the message and brief. A copy-only task can end with a concise visual direction rather than artwork.

## Deliver a traceable result

Adapt the format to the task. A multi-angle or multi-use-case record can contain:

- Stable concept ID, use case and buyer role.
- Exact source excerpt, source link/location and evidence type.
- Buyer tension and intended angle.
- Headline, useful alternatives and supporting copy.
- Paid caption when requested; experience or visual direction when relevant.
- Claim boundaries and review status, outside the customer-facing ad.

Keep research separate from polished copy so the user can distinguish what was said from what was written. Preserve IDs and prior versions through revisions; update captions, source mappings and previews with artwork changes. Make requested coverage inspectable.

Before delivery, check for a recognizable buyer tension, supported product role, distinct reason to test and source trail. Preserve the denominator, cohort and conditions of statistics. A buyer's target is not a result; out-of-hours demand is not an out-of-hours resolution rate.

Return completed work or usable artifact links. Report missing coverage or unavailable capabilities plainly. Do not claim production, publication or full-source review that did not occur.
