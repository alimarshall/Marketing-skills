# Evidence to angles

Read when analysing sources, choosing excerpts or mapping use cases.

## Extract the working situation

Look for a sequence:

> Trigger → current workaround → consequence → desired change.

The Friday spreadsheet, an approval lost in email, a receipt reattached to another form and a customer repeating their story usually create stronger angles than “amazing” or “we need AI.” Enthusiasm matters when you can identify what the person actually saw.

A lightweight evidence record can include:

| Field | Purpose |
| --- | --- |
| Evidence ID | A stable reference through revisions |
| Source and locator | URL, section, transcript timestamp or file line |
| Exact excerpt | Wording actually present in the source |
| Context | The task, feature or question under discussion |
| Buyer / use case | Who cares and what job is involved |
| Evidence type | Pain, aspiration, objection, scoped demonstration or outcome |
| Tension / stakes | Why this matters in the buyer's working day |
| Supported response | What is verified, and by which capability source |
| Boundary | What the evidence does not establish |

Not every brief needs every column. Retain enough to explain why the resulting ad is credible.

## Quote fidelity

- Check the excerpt against the original. Do not reconstruct it from memory or a researcher's summary.
- A substring can be useful, but preserve enough context to avoid changing meaning. Indicate consequential omissions.
- Label transcript cleanup or a shortened paraphrase as edited/adapted. Keep the untouched wording in the evidence bank.
- A researcher's narrative is synthesis, not a direct buyer quotation.
- Keep attribution status separate from message development. Internal evidence can inspire brand copy without becoming a testimonial.
- Do not copy confidential context or identifiable customer details into shareable artwork merely because they were available.

Exact-string checks can help verify a large bank, but cannot establish context, speaker identity or a product claim's truth. Review those separately.

## Reconcile relevance and product truth

Buyer research establishes that a person struggles with, values or wants something. Capability evidence establishes that this company can deliver the advertised response under the stated conditions. Do not substitute one for the other.

A request for predictive insight does not establish a prediction feature. One connected demo does not establish every integration. A customer's result does not establish a universal guarantee.

If evidence conflicts, record the difference and narrow the message. A product may coordinate around another tool without replacing it. Advertise the useful job it performs rather than the desired replacement.

Use a proportional classification:

- **Ready to draft:** relevant pain and supported product response.
- **Scoped / example-dependent:** useful with known connection, configuration or review conditions.
- **Exploratory:** buyer desire whose product response is not established.

Keep this classification in review notes. Rather than loading the public copy with caveats, choose a truthful promise it can communicate clearly.

## Use-case coverage

Read each requested use case rather than applying a company-wide slogan mechanically. Map the trigger, friction, artifact/systems, owner, supported next action and benefit.

For example: a customer reports the wrong item → details are re-entered between support and warehouse → order, photos and case need to travel together → the warehouse owner receives the exception with context → less relaying and a clear owner.

Choose breadth based on material. Several pains can justify several concepts. A thin source may support one strong angle and an exploratory direction rather than equal production volume everywhere.

For “every excerpt,” map each excerpt to a direction and note when several support one hypothesis. For “each use case,” cover each requested case and state how many routes were selected. Do not claim every quotation was covered unless it was.

## Check the angle

A reasoning aid:

> For [buyer], when [moment], [friction] makes [consequence] harder; [product action] enables [desired progress].

Rewrite naturally for the ad; do not force this syntax into customer-facing copy.

Revise an angle that could sell almost any product after changing the logo, depends on an unverified wish, names a feature without stakes, overstates the demonstrated outcome, or differs from another route only in wording.

Evidence breadth and emotional intensity are editorial signals, not conversion predictions. Separate repeated calls from independent demand. Do not claim statistical significance from a qualitative collection.
