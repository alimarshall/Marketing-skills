# Writing and examples

Read when turning a researched tension into headlines, supporting lines and captions.

## Voice and structure

Sound like a perceptive colleague who understands the work: clear, specific and commercially useful. Avoid inflated claims, jargon and generic inspiration.

Research supplies vocabulary, not finished copy. Preserve recognizable nouns and stakes while removing verbal filler, rambling setup and unnecessary hostility.

### Headline

Choose one main idea. Useful shapes include a recognizable situation, a concrete change, an aspiration with an audience, a specific question, or verified proof followed by its meaning.

A short two-beat headline can work, but it is not a compulsory style. Use one line, an explanatory headline or another cadence when the brand and placement call for it.

Avoid “Move with intent,” “Make room for more,” “Own what’s next” or “Your controls. Your approvals.” unless the full asset immediately explains the audience, work and value. Punchier should mean clearer or more memorable, not emptier.

### Supporting line

Answer the next question: what does the product do here?

Connect action to useful change. Name the product/category when needed for a cold audience. Do not repeat the headline or list every feature. A platform message can span connected jobs; a use-case ad should show the particular job well.

Controls, architecture and configuration can reassure. They should lead only when they are the actual purchase concern or buyer's job.

### Paid caption

Add context rather than copying the artwork:

1. Identify the situation or cost of the workaround.
2. Explain the supported product role.
3. Clarify the benefit or next step.

Scale length to the channel and requested deliverable. Add a CTA when the objective, format or user calls for one. Neither CTA-free artwork nor a CTA on every asset is a universal rule.

## Distinct routes

One supported workflow can be tested through recognition, progress, capacity, confidence or strategic scope. Use only routes grounded in the research. Do not manufacture a fear-of-missing-out story from a convenience feature. Useful urgency comes from an actual deadline, recurring cycle or stated mandate.

## Fictional worked examples

The quotes and capabilities below are invented teaching examples, not research to reuse as customer evidence. Replace them with verified material in real work. `{Product}` is a placeholder for the current company.

### Returns operations

**Invented excerpt:** “Every exception goes from support to the warehouse, then comes back asking for the order details again.”

**Assumed capability:** create and assign a returns case with order context and keep the conversation together. Warehouse staff make the fulfilment decision.

**Recognition:** “Another return. The same details, entered again.”

**Progress:** “The return needs attention. The next owner has the context.”

**Capacity:** “Give support room for the customer, not the relay.”

**Supporting line:** “{Product} brings the order, return details and next owner into one connected case.”

**Caption:** “A returns exception should not mean rebuilding the story for every team. {Product} carries the order details and conversation into the warehouse handoff, so the next person can start with the work.”

**Experience:** Customer reports an incorrect item → product attaches the order and assigns the case → warehouse reviewer confirms the replacement decision. Do not show an automatic refund or shipment unless supported.

### Revenue operations reporting

**Invented excerpt:** “Before every leadership meeting I’m rebuilding the same account report from three exports.”

**Assumed capability:** generate an account report from connected CRM data; no verified native slide export.

**Recognition:** “The leadership meeting is Monday. The report rebuild starts Friday.”

**Progress:** “Ask the account question. Get the customer view.”

**Strategic:** “Give revenue teams a clearer view of the next decision.”

**Supporting line:** “{Product} turns questions about connected account data into a report your team can work with.”

**Boundary:** Do not promise an automatically finished presentation or predicted churn without evidence.

### Cross-channel service

**Invented excerpt:** “The customer emails us, then calls, and has to explain the whole thing again.”

**Assumed capability:** combine verified customer context and linked conversations for an assigned agent; channel coverage is known.

**Recognition:** “The channel changed. The customer’s story shouldn’t start again.”

**Progress:** “A new message. The context already there.”

**Supporting line:** “{Product} brings connected conversations and customer context into the agent’s case.”

**Boundary:** “One front door” describes the supported experience, not every possible channel.

### Consumer product

**Invented excerpt:** “I stopped carrying the bottle because it leaked in my bag.”

**Assumed capability:** a reusable bottle has a verified lid seal under documented test conditions.

**Recognition:** “Your bag has enough in it. A spill shouldn’t be one of them.”

**Progress:** “A bottle made for the daily carry.”

**Supporting line:** Describe the verified lid feature and practical use in the current brand's voice. Do not turn a limited test into an unconditional “never leaks” promise. Use the actual product and a relevant demonstration, not a chat interface.

### Local service

**Invented excerpt:** “I can’t block an entire day waiting for a repair visit.”

**Assumed capability:** the service offers confirmed two-hour arrival windows in specified areas.

**Recognition:** “The repair needs a visit. It shouldn’t need your whole day.”

**Supporting line:** “{Company} offers confirmed two-hour arrival windows in participating service areas.”

**Boundary:** Do not imply an on-time guarantee or wider geographic coverage without evidence. Make the service and practical customer situation visible in the creative.

## Final edit

Read the complete asset and caption together. Can a cold reader tell whose problem this is, what changes and what the offering does? Is the distinctive buyer detail still present? Does the supporting line add meaning? Is an aspiration being dressed up as proof? Do alternatives test different ideas? Does it sound like the current brand rather than a previous client?

Read aloud. Cut unnecessary setup, abstract verbs and repetition. Preserve the nouns that make the work recognizable.
