# Creative and review

Read for visual directions, artwork or a review library. This is optional, not a required production mode.

## Let the message choose the treatment

| Message | Useful treatments | Keep clear |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Daily frustration or completed task | Product experience, working moment, typography | The request and meaningful outcome |
| A function's ambition | People, workplace or brand imagery; selective product overlay | Audience and credible new scope |
| Governance or architecture | Authentic product surfaces or a purposeful technical drawing | The actual concern; explain rather than decorate |
| Quantified proof | A stat followed by its meaning | Metric, cohort and conditions |
| Cross-system work | A short sequence across relevant systems | Each person/system's contribution |

Avoid defaulting everything to one diagram, photograph or transcript. A controlled layout is useful for a deliberate copy test; record that choice rather than calling it a new visual hypothesis.

## Product experiences

Use the company's current website, approved screenshots, product documentation, design files or repository where available. Match actual identity treatment, message structure, terminology, status states, reports and controls. Do not invent a dashboard merely because it looks like software.

Distinguish in review notes an actual screenshot, a source-style composition for a fictional example, and a conceptual illustration of a proposed experience.

Show only the sequence the message needs:

> Request → action/review → outcome or named handoff.

For cross-system orchestration, show the dependencies rather than one “follow-up sent” message. For a balance question, the answer may be the whole completed experience. Do not add steps just to make the product look busier.

Keep states accurate: draft ready is not approved or sent; approval requested is not received; specialist assigned is a handoff, not necessarily resolution; claim queued is not payment made; displayed data is not a validated forecast.

Human review must precede actions that depend on it. Follow the product's actual messaging conventions; do not impose a previous campaign's left/right alignment preference on every company.

## Photography and overlays

Follow the user's preference. Genuine stock or supplied photographs are strong choices for natural human realism. Generate imagery when requested or useful, record its origin and retain approved exceptions explicitly. Never imply a generated or stock person is a real customer or endorser.

For UI on photography, protect faces and the meaningful action, make type readable at intended mobile size, and integrate the surface through placement and contrast. Use subject masks or depth sparingly: never hide names or message text, or leave broken silhouette edges. Avoid repeated frames and unnecessary nesting. Check each aspect ratio separately.

Do not carry over another company's glass effect, beige background, serif font, logo, app badges or photography as a default brand system.

## Evidence on the artwork

Native product controls can demonstrate an experience. Promotional CTAs depend on the brief. Keep internal attribution, source notes and capability boundaries outside the artwork unless the message or disclosure requires them.

A statistic can lead when proof is the angle. Preserve what was measured: arrivals outside working hours cannot become resolutions outside working hours. A customer's result should not silently become a product-wide result.

Competitor references can inform hierarchy, density, photographic approach or rhythm. Translate the mechanism into the current identity and product truth rather than copying claims or implying association.

## Review and iteration

For many assets, use stable IDs and group by theme, buyer or use case. Connect artwork, caption, source and rationale in an inspectable review surface. In-page previews are useful when available. Preserve history where comparison matters; default to the current round as a library grows.

Replace every active use of a rejected image or direction. Keep clearly historical versions only where version history is intended. Refresh contact sheets, previews, captions and source/copy links alongside the individual asset.

Check actual deliverables proportionally: legibility, clipping, overlap, missing assets, aspect ratios, approval order, source mapping and coverage. A preview check does not prove product accuracy or campaign effectiveness.

Without visual production tools, return finished copy and a usable creative brief. Do not fabricate screenshots, export files or publication status.
