# Research method: getting from buyer evidence to angle candidates

Angles are extracted, not authored. This file is the extraction method. It assumes the ground truth in your input documents (see `inputs.md`) is already open.

**Read `pain-mapping.md` first if you are starting from raw material.** That file covers the layer underneath this one: the five extraction buckets, signal scoring, verbatim discipline, the pain by avatar matrix, and the corpora most teams are not yet mining. This file picks up once the material is extracted, and sorts it into angle candidates. And read `ad-avatars.md` before either, because the avatars are what the pains get sorted against.

The single discipline that matters: **keep the buyer's words**. The moment a verbatim becomes your paraphrase it loses the thing that made it useful, which is that it was said by someone with no interest in making you sound good.

---

## 1. What to pull, and from where

Check what research already exists before commissioning any. Where a programme is well supplied, the job is retrieval and sorting, not fieldwork.

| Looking for | Go to |
|---|---|
| Pain language, in buyer words | Your buyer-research documents, and any voice-of-customer emotion and pain-language doc (see `inputs.md`) |
| Aspiration and excitement language | The aspiration and interests sections of your buyer research |
| Frequency-ranked themes | Any ranked pain synthesis your team maintains, per product line |
| Objections and anxieties | Competitor dossiers and their deal-intelligence sections; the gap rows of your claim verification tracker |
| What you already claim, and at what tier | Your claims or product communication matrix |
| What buyers type into a search box | Your keyword and SEO research |
| Fresh material since the last sweep | Your recurring research sweep, or the call-recording corpus directly |
| Already-extracted pains, triggers, objections, verbatims, by avatar | The pain bank. Check here before re-mining anything. |
| The avatars themselves | The avatar library |

If a job needs a pain that none of these contain, that is a research finding, not a licence to invent one. Write it as a gap.

**Consent travels with the quote.** Anything from a call recording, win/loss or a named customer is **INTERNAL USE ONLY** unless there is explicit sign-off. It can shape an angle, inform a hook, and appear in the brief's evidence section under that banner. It cannot appear in an ad. Public case studies and published sources are the exception.

---

## 2. Sort by the four forces

Every quote you pull goes into one of four buckets. This is Bob Moesta's switch model, and it is the most useful sorting frame for ad angles because each force produces a structurally different argument.

| Force | The question it answers | The angle family it produces |
|---|---|---|
| **Push** | What is bad enough about today that they started looking | Pain-led, cost-of-inaction, time-drain, risk. The default, and usually the most crowded lane. |
| **Pull** | What does the better world look like, specifically | Outcome, aspiration, identity, speed-to-value. Consistently under-used in B2B and the closest fit to a growth-led register. |
| **Anxiety** | What could go wrong if they switch to you | Objection-killer, switching-cost demolition, proof, security and containment. Where B2B deals actually stall. |
| **Habit** | Why is doing nothing so easy | Status-quo attack, "why now", trigger events. The angle family that addresses no-decision, which loses more deals than any competitor does. |

A switch happens when **Push plus Pull beats Anxiety plus Habit**. Creative that only pushes is arguing one term of a four-term inequality.

Two consequences of this sorting worth drawing out on any run:

- **Anxiety is usually the live constraint on an AI or data-access story.** Where the leading recorded objection is containment rather than capability (permissions, whether it trains on our data, the panic-delete scenario), that is an Anxiety-force problem, and it means security proof belongs high in the creative rather than in a footer. Capability angles that ignore it are arguing the wrong term. Take the objection ranking from your own research rather than assuming it.
- **Push often has a register ceiling.** Where your brand rules forbid building a piece on operational pain, or contrasting "administering" against "leading", Push evidence is allowed as a named operational specific stated once, flatly, and not as the emotional centre. In practice this pushes you toward Pull and Anxiety angles more than a generic B2B playbook would, which is a real advantage: those lanes are emptier.

## 3. Fix the awareness state

Eugene Schwartz's five levels, from *Breakthrough Advertising*. This is the highest-leverage decision in the whole workflow, because it determines what the ad is allowed to assume.

| State | What they know | What the ad opens with | Never open with | Typical LinkedIn audience |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **Unaware** | Not aware they have this problem | A specific observation, a number, an identity call-out. No product. | The product name | Cold, broad job-function and seniority |
| **Problem-aware** | Feels the pain, does not know the category solves it | The pain named precisely, and what it costs | A feature | Cold ICP, skills and function targeting |
| **Solution-aware** | Knows the category exists, has not picked | The mechanism. How this actually works, and why that shape | Generic category benefits everyone claims | Warm, content and video engagers |
| **Product-aware** | Knows you, not convinced | Proof, comparison, the objection they are actually holding | The problem they solved months ago | Site retargeting, engagement retargeting |
| **Most aware** | Ready, needs a reason to move now | The offer, the trigger, the low-friction path | Education | Demo-page and pricing-page retargeting |

Two failures account for most wasted B2B spend, and both are awareness mismatches: cold creative that opens with a product name, and retargeting creative that opens with a generic pain the viewer moved past three weeks ago.

**Market sophistication is a separate axis** and gets conflated with awareness constantly. Awareness is what this buyer knows about their problem. Sophistication is how tired the whole market is of hearing the claim. Place your own category on it explicitly, and write down where, because the placement changes what a claim is worth rather than just how it sounds.

- **Established category claims usually sit late.** In a mature category the umbrella claims, the "all-in-one platform" and "built for modern teams" formulations have been said by everyone for a decade. At that sophistication a bare claim carries no information. What still works: a mechanism, a specific number with its denominator, or a reframe of what the category is for.
- **New capability claims sit earlier and age fast.** Direct capability claims still land while a technology is new, but buyer scepticism compounds. Where a technology label has already become a discount trigger for your buyers, your own writing rules on that label are the operative guidance and they outrank anything here.

A claim that would work at sophistication 2 and is deployed at sophistication 5 does not read as bold. It reads as noise.

## 4. Build the angle candidates

Work the grid deliberately. For each persona in the buying committee, at each awareness state in scope, across all four forces, ask what argument the evidence supports.

Work it against the **avatars** in your avatar library, not against the generic committee table below. The table is the underlying frame; the avatars are the evidenced instances of it, and they carry the verbatim language and the objections the table does not.

The generic buying committee, to be replaced by the seats your own research names:

| Seat | What moves them | Force they usually sit on |
|---|---|---|
| Economic buyer (function head, CFO) | The cost of the current way, in their numbers | Push, Habit |
| Practitioner (the person in the workflow daily) | The before and after of their actual workflow | Push, Pull |
| Technical / IT gatekeeper | Integration and security specifics, the permission model | Anxiety |
| Security / compliance | The audit trail, the human-oversight design | Anxiety |

**One story, branched proof.** The same mechanism runs through every seat. Only the evidence and the unit of measurement change. If a persona-specific line cannot be traced back to the positioning, it is drift, not tailoring.

Target **eight to fifteen distinct angle territories** before narrowing. Distinct means a different argument, not a different sentence. If two candidates would be answered by the same objection, they are one territory.

---

## 5. Score, then cut

Three filters, in this order. Evidence first is deliberate: a differentiated angle with no buyer evidence is a hypothesis about the market, not about the buyer.

**Evidence confidence**

| Tier | Test |
|---|---|
| High | Multiple named accounts, recent, in the buyer's own words, consistent across sources |
| Medium | One or two accounts, or older, or paraphrased in a synthesis doc rather than quoted |
| Low | Pattern-level or unattributed. Usable as a supporting note, never as the basis of a lead angle |

**Differentiation**: where it sits on the share-of-voice grid from the teardown. Saturated (three or more competitors claiming it), contested (one or two), or open (zero).

**Proof strength**, ranked: named metric with denominator > named customer story > demo or screenshot > third-party validation > mechanism explanation. Mechanism is the only one available before customers exist, and the one technical buyers weight most. Check the ranking against your own writing rules (see `inputs.md`) in case yours orders them differently.

Then apply the matching rule: **proof strength scales with claim strength.** If the proof is not there, shrink the claim until it is. A smaller specific claim outperforms a bigger vague one because it survives the buyer's checklist.

Cut to three to five. Record what you cut and why, separating evidence cuts from register cuts. The register cuts in particular are worth keeping, because they will be proposed again in six months.

---

## 6. Angles that come from somewhere other than pain

Three sources worth checking explicitly, because pain mining alone misses them.

- **The demotions.** Your positioning work names what was demoted and why. A capability demoted from Lead because it is table stakes in the category can still be a strong ad angle at a different awareness state, where the buyer does not yet know it is table stakes.
- **The gaps you admit.** The product-gap rows of your claim verification tracker, and the "where we trail" section of your competitive differentiation notes. Naming a limitation is one of the cheapest credibility purchases available and almost nobody does it. An honest scope statement outperforms a hedge: substitute, do not hedge.
- **Trigger events.** Funding round, a merger, a new market, a first international hire, an audit, a CFO asking for headcount justification. These are Habit-force angles and they are the only ones that answer "why now". They are also directly targetable on LinkedIn in a way most angles are not.

## 7. Where this hands off

The output of this file is a scored, cut list of angle candidates with their evidence attached. That list goes into:

- `angle-library.md` to check taxonomy fit, execution patterns and register collisions.
- `competitor-teardown.md` Step 4 to get the share-of-voice read that the differentiation filter needs.
- `creative-matrix.md` section 2 to be deployed across the five awareness states, and section 4 to be priced.
- `hooks-and-mechanics.md` and SKILL.md Step 8 to be turned into concepts.

If you find yourself unable to complete the four-force sort because the research does not cover this audience, stop and say so. Run a fresh research sweep, or scope the brief down to the audience the research does cover. A concept set built on assumed pain is worse than no concept set, because it will be tested, it will fail, and the team will conclude the channel does not work.
