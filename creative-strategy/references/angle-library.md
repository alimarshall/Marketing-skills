# Angle library

An angle is the argument the ad makes. Not the format, not the hook, not the execution.

```
Angle      the argument            lives for months, underlies many ads
Concept    angle made specific     persona + format + hook + visual + proof
Hook       the first line or frame that gets the angle read
Format     the structural shape it is poured into
Execution  the finished file
```

Teams conflate angle and execution constantly, then report "we tested twelve creatives" when they tested one argument twelve ways. A strong angle survives weak hook execution. A clever hook cannot rescue a flawed angle.

**This library is a menu, not a permission slip.** In most brands several of the most standard B2B angles are unusable, and several more are usable only in a specific shape. Section 3 covers the collisions between standard practice and brand rules. Read it before writing.

---

**Three companions to this file.** `hooks-and-mechanics.md` carries the layers below the angle: the eight creative mechanics, the hook tactics with their availability marks, and the awareness-matched opening rules. `creative-matrix.md` section 2 carries the deployment grid, which is where an angle gets expressed at each of the five awareness states rather than assigned to one. And your standing angle library, the live doc this skill maintains, carries the live state: which territory is in test, in market, or retired, and the dated share-of-voice read behind each. This file is the taxonomy. That one is the state. Do not confuse them.

## 1. The taxonomy

Grouped by the force from `research-method.md` that each angle recruits.

### Push angles: what hurts now

| Angle | The argument | Fits | Usually executed as | Notes |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **Cost of inaction** | Waiting has a price, here it is | Problem-aware, stalled deals, no-decision | Stat card, receipt or invoice format, calculator | Loss framing is the strongest answer to a stalled deal. Needs a real number, which means your claim verification tracker gates it |
| **Hidden cost** | You are already paying for this, you just cannot see it | Problem-aware | Receipt, itemised list, annotated screenshot | Works where the cost is real but unbudgeted. Distinct from time drain: this is money, not hours |
| **Time drain** | X hours a week, gone | Problem-aware, practitioner | Big-number card, before and after | Often register-constrained. See 3.1. Where it is, the allowed version is one flat operational specific, never the emotional centre |
| **Risk and compliance** | The exposure you are carrying | Regulated, multi-country, audit-adjacent | Spec sheet, checklist, quote from a named regulation | Strong in EU and multi-entity contexts. Must stay factual. Fear-first execution reads as defensive and downmarket, which most enterprise registers reject |
| **Fragmented stack** | The problem is that these do not talk to each other | Solution-aware, ops leaders | Workflow diagram, integration wall, before and after | The consolidation lane. Heavily contested in most mature categories. Only worth entering with a specific mechanism |
| **Data you cannot trust** | Your record is wrong and every downstream thing inherits it | Problem to solution-aware, analytical buyers | Annotated screenshot, org chart, data-viz | Underclaimed in most categories, and close to what a system-of-record product actually leads on |

### Pull angles: what they want

| Angle | The argument | Fits | Usually executed as | Notes |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **Outcome / ROI** | Here is the result, in your numbers | Product-aware, economic buyer | Metric card, calculator, case-study quote | Proof-gated. Shrink the claim until the proof carries it |
| **Speed to value** | Live in weeks, not quarters | Product-aware, buyers burned by implementation | Timeline graphic, stat card | Blocked wherever your implementation timeline has conflicting internal sources. Settle the number before it ships, do not average the sources |
| **Capacity, not headcount** | The work gets done without another hire | Economic buyer, automation and AI products | Workflow diagram, day-in-the-life, agent-run narrative | Usually the correct framing for automation. Never headcount replacement, which invites a quality comparison you lose on edge cases and anchors price to a salary |
| **Identity / built for you** | This was made for your exact situation | Cold, imprecise targeting | ICP call-out headline, job-ad format | Specificity filters the audience better than targeting settings do |
| **Built for X, not Y** | Explicitly not for the other kind of company | Cold, ICP-narrow | Comparison table without competitor names, checklist | The safe form of a comparison ad under most brand rules: contrast against a *type* of product, not a vendor |
| **Ambition / leading** | What becomes possible when this function is a growth engine | Any, especially senior | Founder-face, thought leader post, big statement | The home lane for registers that lead with growth and ambition. Confirm it is still open against the dated read in your live angle library before treating it as empty |
| **Insider practice** | What the teams who are good at this actually do | Solution-aware, sophisticated | Listicle carousel, document ad, thought leader post | Earns attention without a product claim. Strong top-of-funnel for a tired market |

### Anxiety angles: what could go wrong if they switch

| Angle | The argument | Fits | Usually executed as | Notes |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **Containment and permissions** | Here is exactly what it can and cannot touch | Product-aware, IT, security, any AI campaign | Permission-model diagram, spec sheet, audit-trail screenshot | Frequently the most valuable open lane for an AI product, because the leading objection to AI is usually containment rather than capability and almost nobody advertises against it. Crowding differs by adjacency: it can be open in your category and contested in the AI-governance and security advertising reaching the same IT gatekeeper. Take the dated read from your live angle library, not from this line |
| **What it will never decide** | The high-stakes decisions are not its call | Any AI campaign | Plain statement card, boundary diagram | Explicitly excluding decisions buys more trust than any capability claim |
| **Autonomy as a dial** | You choose: read-only, reversible, notify, mandatory approval | Solution to product-aware, IT | Four-step diagram, slider visual | Resolves the autonomy versus trust tension without weakening either side |
| **Switching cost demolition** | The migration is the objection, here is what happens to it | Product-aware, incumbent-locked | Timeline, checklist, named migration story | Needs a real migration story. Check consent before naming anyone |
| **Objection killer** | The thing you are quietly worried about, answered | Mid to late funnel | Question headline plus one-line answer card | Naming the objection out loud signals confidence. Pairs with retargeting |
| **Proof and peers** | Teams like yours already did this | Solution to product-aware | Quote card, logo wall, review badge | Match proof type to temperature: volume proof cold, named specificity warm, mechanism for technical |
| **Honest scope** | Here is what this covers and what it does not | Sophisticated, technical, late funnel | Plain spec statement | Naming a limitation is the cheapest credibility available and almost nobody does it. Substitute, do not hedge |

### Habit angles: why doing nothing is easy

| Angle | The argument | Fits | Usually executed as | Notes |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **Trigger event** | Something just changed for you, and it changes this | Any, targeted at the trigger | Direct call-out, scenario card | Funding, merger, first international hire, audit, headcount freeze. The only angle family that answers "why now", and LinkedIn can actually target several of these |
| **Status quo attack** | The way this has always been done is the problem | Unaware to problem-aware | Contrarian statement, tier list, meme | Attacks the practice, never a vendor. This is the on-register form of a competitive angle |
| **Contrarian thesis** | We think the accepted view here is wrong | Sophisticated, saturated market | Thought leader post, founder-face, opinion card | Usually has to be a first-person thesis about your own approach. See 3.2 |
| **Category reframe** | This is not what you think it is | Unaware, high sophistication | Big statement plus mechanism | High risk. Do not invent a category in an ad. Reframing what an existing category is *for* is the achievable version |
| **Your CFO will ask** | Here is the answer to the question finance will put to you | Champion enablement, mid funnel | Objection card, one-page ROI, document ad | Arms the internal champion. Under-used, and it addresses the real blocker in mid-market deals, which is internal selling |

### Angles that exist in the market and are commonly unavailable

Listed so they are recognised in a teardown, not so they are used.

**Fill this table in for your own brand on first use.** Take each row, check it against your brand voice and writing rules, your legal and substantiation policy, and your consent position (see `inputs.md`), then write down whether it is available, available in a narrow shape, or out, and the permitted neighbour if it is out. Doing that once is what converts a set of brand rules into a set of creative decisions. Left undone, the rules get discovered late, in a review, after the concepts are built.

| Angle | Constraint category that commonly blocks it | The permitted neighbour |
|---|---|---|
| **Competitor conquest, named** | Register or brand rule; sometimes legal | State your own strength positively. If a genuinely comparative ad is wanted, that is a decision to escalate to whoever owns the brand, not one to make inside a brief |
| **Villain framing at a vendor** | Register or brand rule | Villain framing at an abstract practice or process, which is not a vendor |
| **Anti-incumbent-bloat** | Register or brand rule. Standard for challengers, and it characterises other vendors by implication | "Built for X, not Y" against a product *type* or an architecture |
| **Market-movement framing** | Register or brand rule. "The market moved from X to Y", "everyone shipped an agent this year" both characterise other vendors without naming one | The bare number, or a first-person statement about your own approach |
| **Admin-versus-leading contrast** | Register or brand rule. Common where the brand leads on growth and ambition | One flat operational specific, then the resolution in the growth frame. See 3.1 |
| **Headcount replacement** | Register or brand rule, and often a standing AI-specific rule | Capacity, not headcount |
| **Superlatives and urgency without a real deadline** | Legal or substantiation, plus platform policy. Unverifiable superlatives are among the most common ad rejection triggers | A specific number with its denominator, or a real dated event |
| **Named customer quotes and logos** | Consent | Aggregate findings, public case studies, published sources. See 3.3 |
| **Bare category-umbrella claims** | Market sophistication. Not forbidden, just inert once everyone has said it for a decade | A mechanism, a specific number with its denominator, or a reframe of what the category is for |

## 2. Angle steers per product line

Record the settled lead story for each product line before generating, and do not re-litigate it inside a creative brief without saying that is what you are doing. A system-of-record product and an automation product built on it are two different arguments even when they share a buyer.

Two checks that should shape angle selection more than they usually do:

- **Run the substitution test on capability angles.** If a buyer can answer your angle with "we could just use a general-purpose tool for that", the angle is arguing on ground where you lose. Angles built on what the product *does*, to which system, with what permission, survive substitution. Angles whose success metric is the quality of an answer often do not. Take the read from live buyer reaction in your research, not from an assumption about what sounds impressive.
- **Separate differentiators from gap-closure.** A capability where you merely reach parity should not lead as a differentiator. Position it as gap-closure: what it stops you losing on. The honest framing is "this removes an objection", not "this is why you switch". And check where the emotion actually sits: for many features it is not in the feature but in what happens either side of it, the analysis afterwards or the place the output lands.

Then take the best-evidenced angle territory from the strongest recorded emotional reactions in your buyer research, and write down which those are, so the next run starts where this one finished.

## 3. The register collisions, and how to resolve them

These are the three places where standard B2B practice and standing brand rules meet. Resolve them. Do not quietly drop the angle and do not quietly break the rule.

### 3.1 Pain-led angles and a growth-led register

Many B2B brands cap how far pain-led creative may go: lead with growth, leading and ambition, not with admin. Where yours does, operational pain may appear as grounding evidence or a specific detail. It is never the throughline, the emotional centre, or a contrast device aimed at the reader.

The practical consequence for ads, which are short and mostly *are* their emotional centre:

- **Allowed**: one named operational specific, stated flatly, followed immediately by the resolution carrying the growth frame. "Nine systems to update when someone changes team." Then move.
- **Not allowed**: dwelling on it. Which means the *agitate* beat of PAS and the *before* half of Before-After-Bridge, as they are normally written, are both out. State the specific once and go.
- **Not allowed**: any construction implying the reader currently administers rather than leads.

This is a real constraint and it is also an advantage. Push creative is saturated in most categories. Take the current read from your dated share-of-voice grid, not from here. Being pushed toward Pull and Anxiety angles moves you into emptier space.

### 3.2 Contrarian and counter-narrative angles, and vendor characterisation

Many B2B brands prohibit characterising competitors in customer-facing copy, named or unnamed, negatively or positively. Where yours does, this angle is unavailable in its usual form and the permitted neighbour is stating your own strength positively.

The swap test: if the sentence still works with "our competitors" silently inserted as its subject, it is out.

- **Allowed**: a first-person thesis about your own approach. "We do not think capability is the hard problem here. Explanation is."
- **Not allowed**: any claim about what others do, what the market did, or what everyone shipped, even with nobody named.
- **Allowed**: attacking a practice, a process, or the status quo, which are not vendors.

### 3.3 Proof, quotes and consent

Every angle in the proof and peers family runs into consent. Anything from call recordings, win/loss or a named customer is internal-use-only without explicit sign-off. Case studies and public sources are the exception.

In a brief this means two clearly separated tiers when the material requires it: a prospect-safe version built on aggregate findings, and a banner-flagged internal-only version. Never blended. In practice, most quote-card concepts need either a sign-off request attached or a rewrite onto public material before they can ship.

## 4. Using this library

**Generating.** Work the grid: each persona, at each awareness state in scope, across all four forces. Aim for eight to fifteen distinct territories before narrowing. If two candidates would be knocked down by the same objection, they are one territory.

**Naming.** Use these names in the swipe file and the share-of-voice grid so the vocabulary is shared across runs, briefs and teardowns. If a competitor cluster does not map to anything here, name the new territory, define it, and add it.

**Scoring.** Evidence, then differentiation, then proof, per `research-method.md` section 5. Cut to three to five. Record what you cut and why, separating evidence cuts from register cuts.

**Maintaining.** Your live angle library is the per-brand record: the argument in one line, its evidence tier, the share-of-voice read at the time, the register check, and status (`Untested`, `In test`, `In market`, `Winner`, `Retired, reason`). This file is the taxonomy. That one is the state.

The retired list is the most valuable part of the live record, because a retired angle with a good story behind it will be proposed again within two quarters by someone who was not there.
