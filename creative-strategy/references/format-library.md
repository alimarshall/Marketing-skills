# Format library

The format is the structural shape the argument gets poured into. Independent of the angle: a hidden-cost angle can be a receipt, a stat card or a quote card, and the choice changes performance more than the wording does.

Two rules govern selection.

**The format has to be the argument's natural shape.** A comparison argument wants a table. A mechanism argument wants a diagram. A credibility argument wants a face or a screenshot. Picking a format because it looks good is how a concept set ends up as five stat cards with different numbers.

**LinkedIn is not Meta.** LinkedIn's feed is scanned by people performing a work identity: primed for credibility, status and information, not entertainment. Text-heavy static images work here and are penalised on Meta. Analyst-style graphics and screenshots perform because the audience is reading, not browsing. Polish reads as "ad" on both platforms, but on LinkedIn the winning shape is usually hybrid: production quality good enough to signal competence, framing informal enough to read as a person rather than a campaign.

---

## 1. Static formats

The workhorse. Cheapest to produce, fastest to vary, and on LinkedIn the single-image ad is where most testing happens.

| Format | Shape | Fits which angles | Why it works | Watch |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **Product screenshot with callout** | Real UI, cropped tight, one arrow or badge on one thing | Data you cannot trust, containment, mechanism, capacity | Proof rather than promise. The claim is visible so it cannot be hedged | Must be legible at phone width. One callout, not four. Never a screenshot of an unshipped state |
| **Big-number card** | One number, huge, almost nothing else | Cost of inaction, hidden cost, outcome, time drain | Restraint signals confidence. Oddly specific numbers read as data, round ones read as marketing | Every number passes the verification tracker. State the denominator |
| **Comparison table, no vendor named** | Two columns, this approach versus that approach | Built for X not Y, status quo attack, fragmented stack | Validates a frustration the reader already has without characterising anyone | Your register rules on vendor characterisation apply. Columns are *approaches* or *product types*, never companies |
| **Checklist / spec sheet** | Dense list of specifics, ticked | Risk and compliance, containment, honest scope | Technical and security buyers actively want density. Rewards specificity where consumer design rules would say cut | Genuinely dense, not five bullets with white space |
| **Permission or autonomy diagram** | The four-step dial, or a scope boundary drawn | Containment, autonomy as a dial, what it will never decide | Makes an abstract trust claim spatially concrete. Nobody was found doing this at the last teardown, so confirm against the dated grid before relying on it | Must match the actual product behaviour, not the roadmap |
| **Workflow / org diagram** | Boxes and arrows, before and after | Fragmented stack, capacity, mechanism | Makes an integration or automation claim checkable | Keep to one path. A full architecture diagram is unreadable in feed |
| **Quote card** | Attributed quote, headshot or logo | Proof and peers, outcome | Third-party voice carries where yours does not | Consent gate. Most internal call material cannot ship. Specific quotes only, never "great product" |
| **Review or badge proof** | G2 or Capterra rating and badge | Proof and peers, objection killer | Lowers perceived risk in a category where buyers already know the source | Weak on its own. Pair with a claim |
| **Receipt / itemised list** | Mock invoice of hidden line items | Hidden cost, cost of inaction | Turns abstract waste into something that looks billable | Register check: it can tip into admin-pain-as-throughline. Keep the resolution in frame |
| **Fake chat or Slack message** | Messaging UI with a scripted exchange | Capacity, agent and automated-workflow angles, mechanism | Shows the product inside a context the buyer already trusts. Very strong for anything that lives in a chat tool | Must look like the real integration, not a generic chat mock |
| **Screenshot of a post** | A LinkedIn post or message rendered as an image | Insider practice, contrarian thesis, proof | Borrows native credibility. Common and effective on LinkedIn | Do not fake a post that was not made |
| **Plain statement card** | One sentence, typographic, no imagery | Ambition, contrarian thesis, honest scope, what it will never decide | When the sentence is the idea, anything else dilutes it | The sentence has to actually be good. Nowhere to hide |
| **ICP call-out** | Names the role or company type in the headline | Identity, built for X not Y, trigger event | Specificity filters better than targeting settings do | Match to targeting or it reads as spam |
| **Job ad format** | Mimics a job posting for the work nobody should have to do | Capacity, identity | Recognisable grammar, reframes the product as the hire you do not make | On-brand only if it stays light. Check it against your register rules |
| **Founder or team face** | A person, direct address | Ambition, contrarian thesis, credential | A face reads as a person rather than a campaign. Strongest on LinkedIn | Needs a real point of view, not a stock portrait |
| **Meme** | Recognisable template, category-specific caption | Status quo attack, identity | Recognition and shareability, humanises a stiff category | Riskier from a brand handle than from a person. Careers and social usually get the most room in a tone hierarchy |
| **Integration logo wall** | Grid of recognisable third-party tools | Fragmented stack, switching cost, anxiety | Directly de-risks "will this fit our stack", a top evaluation criterion in most software categories | Only tools that genuinely integrate today |

## 2. Carousel

LinkedIn carousels take 1080x1080 cards with 45-character headlines each. Best for sequential arguments where each card earns the next swipe.

| Pattern | Use |
|---|---|
| Step-by-step mechanism | Solution-aware. Card one states the problem shape, each following card is one step, last card is the proof |
| Before and after across a workflow | Fragmented stack, capacity |
| Numbered listicle | Insider practice, top of funnel |
| Persona split | One card per seat in the buying committee, same mechanism, different evidence |
| Objection sequence | Late funnel. One objection per card, answered |

First card carries almost all the weight. Write it as though the rest may never be seen, because for most viewers it will not be.

## 3. Document ads

A native, swipeable PDF in the feed. The strongest static-adjacent format on LinkedIn by measured CTR, and the practical replacement for the gated ebook, which has been in measurable decline as buyers self-serve research and avoid anything that triggers an SDR sequence.

- 8 to 12 pages is the working range even though the limit is far higher.
- The value has to be complete inside the document. Ungated delivery is the point.
- Best fits: insider practice, honest scope, your CFO will ask, benchmark or data content, and anything where the argument genuinely needs more than a headline.
- The trade: no form fill, so it measures on engagement and downstream influence rather than CPL. Judge it accordingly, or it will be defunded by a metric it was never running on. See `creative-testing`.

## 4. Video

Weighted far more heavily on Meta than on LinkedIn, but it still earns its place here for dwell time and for building retargeting audiences off view percentage.

- Under 30 seconds for cold. Hook in the first three seconds.
- Captions are mandatory. A large majority of LinkedIn video plays run silent.
- Talking head outperforms produced brand film for dwell time in B2B. Studio-quality lighting with informal framing beats both a slick corporate reel and genuinely amateur footage.
- Vertical and square render larger in a mobile feed. 4:5 and 1:1 are the defaults, 9:16 for the placements that take it.
- Video's real strategic value on LinkedIn at B2B budgets is the retargeting audience it builds: viewers at 25, 50, 75 percent are addressable segments, which turns a cold video into a mid-funnel targeting asset.

## 5. Thought Leader Ads

A promoted organic post from a personal profile, keeping the person's name and photo as the visible author. Mechanically distinct enough to be treated as its own format family.

- The post must exist organically on the personal profile first. It cannot be created inside the ad account.
- The person grants the ad account Thought Leader Ads permission under Campaign Manager account assets.
- **Up to 3,000 characters**, against 600 for standard sponsored content. This is the single biggest creative difference: it is the only paid format on the platform where a full argument fits.
- **No headline and no CTA button.** It is the native post plus paid distribution. That makes it weak for direct response and strong for trust.
- Comments and likes accrue to the person, and the halo depends on them actually replying.
- Independent creative analysis puts thought leader engagement rates at roughly double non-thought-leader median. Vendor-reported CTR and CPC advantages are larger and should be treated as vendor-sourced and selection-biased. CTR comparisons are also structurally unreliable here because "see more" expansions on long posts inflate click counts.
- Best run as a sequence of five to seven posts building an arc rather than one-off boosts.

Fits: contrarian thesis, insider practice, ambition, honest scope. This is where a first-person thesis belongs, and it is the format most aligned with a candid register.

If your team has a playbook for organic LinkedIn post craft, that covers writing the post. Use this skill to decide which angle deserves paid distribution behind it.

## 6. Conversation ads and lead gen forms

- **Conversation ads** carry a 300x250 banner and up to five CTA buttons off an opening message. Best for events, multi-offer nudges, and high-intent retargeting where a menu genuinely helps. The older single-CTA Message Ads format is legacy; build new sends as conversation ads.
- **Lead gen forms** overlay any of the main formats. Native forms convert several times higher than sending traffic to a landing page, because the friction of leaving the app disappears. The trade is context: no page to make the argument, so the ad has to carry all of it, and lead quality tends to be lower per lead. Keep to one to three custom questions.

## 7. Formats to skip at B2B budget scale

- **Text ads, spotlight and follower ads.** Desktop right rail only, tiny real estate, very low CTR. Cheap reminder inventory at best.
- **CTV.** No click, brand awareness only, priced for budgets well above a typical B2B creative programme.
- **Sponsored newsletters.** Buys association with a voice rather than a response. Worth revisiting if you build a newsletter worth sponsoring.
- **Accelerate campaigns.** LinkedIn's AI campaign builder generates audience, creative and bidding from a URL. Treat any output as a draft, never as a launch, and never as a substitute for this workflow. It is a fast on-ramp for teams with no creative strategy, which is the opposite of the problem this skill solves.

## 8. Choosing, in practice

Work down this list.

1. **What shape is the argument?** Comparison wants a table. Mechanism wants a diagram. Credibility wants a face, a screenshot or a quote. Scale wants a number. Sequence wants a carousel or a document.
2. **What awareness state?** Cold rewards a single legible idea. Warm and retargeting can carry density, comparison and objection handling, because attention is already earned.
3. **What proof do you have?** Proof type often decides format on its own. A screenshot means a screenshot ad. A number means a number card. No proof means a mechanism diagram, which is the only proof type available before evidence exists.
4. **What has not been tried?** Check the learning log and your swipe file. Format is a legitimate test variable in its own right, and it is usually cheaper to vary than the argument.
5. **Can you actually make it?** A format nobody can produce this week is not a format. `visualisation.md` covers what can be produced without a designer.

Across a concept set, vary the format deliberately. Five variants of one format tests wording. Five formats tests something worth knowing.

Exact dimensions, character limits and file specs are in `platform-specs.md`. Validate every piece of copy against them before delivery.
