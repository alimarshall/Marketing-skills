# Pain mapping: extraction, scoring, and the matrix

`research-method.md` covers the four forces, the awareness ladder and the scoring filters that turn evidence into angle candidates. This file covers the layer underneath it: how raw buyer material gets extracted, scored and organised in the first place, and what is probably not being mined.

Two design rules govern everything here.

**Score for signal before you analyse.** Low-signal material averaged in with high-signal material produces a plausible pain nobody has. The discard is the work.

**Keep the pattern layer and the verbatim layer physically separate.** Patterns drive strategy. Verbatims drive copy. A document that mixes them corrupts both: the patterns acquire false precision from the quotes sitting next to them, and the quotes get read as representative when they were illustrative. Build the pain bank in two halves for this reason, and keep it that way.

---

## 1. The five buckets

Every pass extracts into exactly five buckets, per avatar. Not four, not seven, and not a free-form theme list.

| Bucket | What goes in it | What it is for |
|---|---|---|
| **Pains** | The problem as it was before, in pattern form | Push-force angles. The default, and the crowded one. |
| **Trigger moments** | What finally made them start looking | The only material that answers "why now". Habit-force angles. Often targetable. |
| **Objections** | The scepticism they had to get past, most likely first | Anxiety-force angles. Where B2B deals actually stall. |
| **Transformations** | The specific result and the emotional shift after | Pull-force angles, and the proof layer. |
| **Standout language** | Ad-ready phrasing, by verbatim ID only | Raw material for hooks. Never quoted in the pattern half. |

The bucket set maps onto the four forces cleanly, which is the point: the extraction shape and the angle shape are the same shape, so nothing has to be re-sorted later.

**Triggers and transformations are the two buckets that come back empty most often**, and they are the two that produce the angles nobody else is running. Check transformations specifically for measured outcomes rather than intentions. A figure that looks like an outcome is often a buyer's own target or projection, budgeted hours and headline percentages especially, and a bank full of those has no before-and-after number in it at all. Where that is the state, it is not a gap in the extraction method. It is a gap in what the company has collected, and it caps how strong any proof-led angle can be. Name it in the brief instead of substituting a softer claim.

## 2. Signal scoring

Score every candidate pain 1 to 5 before it earns a place.

| Score | Test |
|---|---|
| **5** | Named account, recent, first-person, specific, in the buyer's own words |
| **4** | Named account, first-person, but paraphrased in a synthesis doc |
| **3** | Named account, but relayed, partial, or single-account |
| **2** | Pattern across accounts, with attribution thin or absent |
| **1** | Unattributed pattern assertion. A number with no denominator or no source. |

**Discard the 1s by name.** Write down which assertions you dropped, because they circulate and they will be proposed again. Watch for the specific case of a round percentage that does have a source and has no denominator and no stated method behind it. Having a citation is not the same as being usable in a headline.

**Apply one rubric, and keep applying it.** This one scores *attribution*, so a thinly attributed cross-country pattern scores below a single named account. Do not re-derive it mid-programme: a second rubric would silently re-rank every entry already in the bank.

Scores 2 and 3 are both usable as supporting notes and neither carries a lead angle on its own, for different reasons. A 3 is one account, so it is a bet on one person's vocabulary, and the sharpest phrases in a bank very often do come from a single speaker at a single account, which is exactly the risk. A 2 is a pattern nobody can trace, so it cannot be checked. A lead angle needs a 4 or a 5.

**Relayed evidence is a distinct failure mode.** Gatekeeper and security avatars are where it usually hides: an avatar can hold a stack of verbatims and almost no gatekeeper voice, because most of them are practitioner-titled or finance-titled people describing the security conversation they carried on someone else's behalf. That is real evidence of how the champion narrates the objection, and it is not evidence of how the gatekeeper states it. Score it accordingly and say which one you have.

## 3. Verbatim discipline

**Quote word for word from the source file. Never from working notes.** This is the single most likely defect in the whole workflow, because the tidied version always reads better, and it has produced real fabrications.

Each banked entry carries: an ID, the exact quote, who said it with title and account where named, source file and location, bucket, avatars it belongs to, consent status, and one line on what makes it usable as raw material.

That last field is worth the effort. "Usable" means it contains something a writer cannot invent: a concrete noun, a number with a denominator, an unexpected verb, or an admission. A quote that only expresses a sentiment is not raw material, it is agreement.

**If you cannot reproduce it exactly, do not bank it.** Most rejections at this gate happen because the source paraphrased rather than quoted. Rejecting them is correct, and the rejection list is itself useful, because it names the accounts worth going back to.

**One conflict shape to expect.** A strong quote can contain something your writing rules forbid: a banned punctuation mark, a banned phrase, a superlative. Reproducing it exactly breaches the writing rule; altering it breaches the verbatim rule. Flag it in the bank for an editorial decision rather than silently resolving it either way. When you hit this shape of conflict, flag it, do not pick.

**Consent tagging.** Default to `INTERNAL USE ONLY`. `PUBLIC OK` requires the source doc to record that the material is published or signed off. Assume nothing is cleared until a source doc says so. See `ad-avatars.md` section 4.

## 4. The pain by avatar matrix

The artefact that makes coverage visible. Pains as rows, avatars as columns, and in each filled cell a few words on *how that avatar experiences it differently*. The differences are the useful part; the ticks alone tell you nothing you did not already know.

Read it two ways.

**Down the rows.** A pain held by most avatars is a candidate for a lead angle, because the same argument can carry across a whole set with only the proof changing. A pain held by one is a candidate for a targeted set, and usually a better one, because specificity is what filters the audience when the targeting cannot.

**Across the columns.** An empty column is a buying-committee seat the creative programme is not addressing. That is the check almost nobody runs, and it is why so many B2B sets are coherent and still cannot move a stalled deal: they are five arguments aimed at the same seat.

Write both reads down after each pass, in this shape. Name the broadest-held territory in the library and say whether creative is currently using it, because the widest row and the least-used lane are frequently the same row. Then name any pain that appears across several rows as the thing anchoring the decision but has no column of its own, which is what happens when an economic-buyer seat, typically the finance signer, has no evidenced avatar. A set built off a matrix in that state addresses the champion and not the signer, and no amount of concept quality fixes it.

**Exclusion is a valid matrix output.** A broadly held pain can be deliberately excluded as a lead angle, most commonly when the sources treat it as a pricing-model question, and no ad answers a pricing-model question. Record the exclusion and the reason in the row rather than leaving a strong-looking candidate unexplained.

## 5. What is probably not being mined, and should be

Most B2B buyer research comes from one corpus shape: your own sales conversations. Call recordings, cold calls, win/loss, pipeline synthesis. That corpus is unusually rich and it has one structural bias that matters for creative: **everyone in it is already talking to you.**

It contains no material from people who never took the call, and almost no unprompted language, because a discovery call is a prompted setting. For angles aimed at cold, problem-aware audiences, unprompted language is exactly what is missing.

Four review and forum corpora fix it, in rough order of value:

| Source | What it gives that call recordings cannot | Note |
|---|---|---|
| **G2, Capterra, TrustRadius** | Unprompted comparison language, and the specific complaints people make about incumbents in public | The buyer's own vocabulary for switching. Where your rules forbid characterising competitors, mine it for your own positive framing, never paraphrase a competitor's negative reviewer language as an insult |
| **Glassdoor and employee-review sites** | The *end users* of software in your category describing the processes your buyer owns | The underexploited one. Your buyer's internal reputation problem, in the words of the people who hold it |
| **Practitioner subreddits and communities** | Unprompted problem statements, before any vendor is involved. Trigger events described as they happen | Closest thing available to the unaware and problem-aware states |
| **Your own support and CS tickets** | What breaks after purchase, which is the objection layer for the next buyer | Internal, so consent is simpler, and almost nobody mines it |

Two more research assets, neither of which is review mining:

**Panel message pre-testing.** At LinkedIn CPMs there is not enough volume to A/B a way to message-market fit. A titled B2B panel returns clarity, relevance, value and differentiation reads on an angle in a day or two, at roughly fifty respondents. It diagnoses why a message fails and does not measure conversion impact, so it substitutes for the in-market read you cannot afford, not for the test. It is usually the highest-leverage research purchase available to a programme at this budget scale, and worth proposing on its own.

**Measured creative failure.** The "what not to say" field across an avatar library is normally built from buyer-stated rejections and register rules. Once the learning log has results in it, that field should be rebuilt from what actually underperformed. Until then, label it as reasoning rather than evidence.

Run a review-mining pass through your recurring research sweep rather than ad hoc inside a creative run, so the output lands in the standing docs and compounds. A creative brief is the wrong place to do fieldwork.

## 6. Handing off

The output of this file is a scored bucket set per avatar, a verbatim bank, and a matrix with its two reads written down.

That goes to `research-method.md` for the four-force sort and the awareness fix, then to `angle-library.md` for taxonomy fit and register collisions, then to `creative-matrix.md` to become a deployment plan.

If the buckets cannot be filled for the audience in scope, stop and say so. Run a fresh research sweep, or narrow the brief to the audience the research covers. A concept set built on assumed pain will be tested, will fail, and the team will conclude the channel does not work.
