# Starting budgets, testing periods and scaling

Use this to propose a new company's LinkedIn media plan or revise an existing one. Keep completed plans and actual performance in that company's private workspace. Use [measurement and optimization](measurement-and-optimization.md) for metric definitions and [launch and change](launch-and-change.md) for authorized execution.

## How to use the ranges

The ranges below are **operator planning assumptions**, not LinkedIn recommendations, observed account results, market benchmarks or promises of lead volume. They are a first proposal to check against the company's economics, audience, account forecast and actual delivery. The test windows and scaling percentages are also adjustable operating heuristics unless explicitly identified as a platform requirement.

All amounts are **USD media spend**, excluding creative production, agency fees, research/enrichment tools, event costs, tax and sales follow-up. Monthly figures mean **30 scheduled delivery days**, calculated as daily budget × 30. Use actual scheduled days for a real month or event flight. A monthly equivalent is not a platform spending cap. For another currency, use the account currency, current local minimums and local auction costs; do not just change the currency symbol.

Each row funds **one audience/offer/market cell**, normally one ad set, across its ads. It is not a separate budget for every creative. With shared campaign budgeting, treat the row as a planning allocation and avoid counting it again as an additional ad-set budget.

## Starting budgets by campaign job

Choose the rows that match the initial program. A company does not need every motion at launch.

| Campaign job | Starting daily budget | 30-day equivalent | Initial evaluation window | What earns more budget |
|---|---:|---:|---|---|
| Cold prospecting: problem/proof-led image or carousel | $50–150 | $1,500–4,500 | 21–28 days | Relevant company/role delivery and useful qualified response; inspect landing-page conversion if that is the goal |
| Cold executive TLA or educational video | $30–100 | $900–3,000 | 21–28 days | Relevant reach, attention appropriate to the content and useful warm-audience growth; downstream quality when observable |
| Warm TLA/video nurture | $15–50 | $450–1,500 | 14–28 days after the pool can deliver | Qualified audience replenishment, healthy reach/frequency and meaningful next-step engagement |
| Ebook/guide acquisition: form, document or landing page | $50–150 | $1,500–4,500 | 21–28 days for delivery/lead quality; longer for meetings/pipeline | Valid ICP-fit leads, working asset delivery and acceptable qualified-outcome costs |
| Direct demo/evaluation/trial acquisition | $75–200 | $2,250–6,000 | 28–42 days, then evaluate matured sales cohorts | Qualified held meetings/evaluations and credible acquisition economics, with enough sales capacity |
| Webinar: cold invitation audience | $75–200 | $2,250–6,000 | Usually a 14–28-day pre-event flight; review attendance afterward | Target-buyer registrations likely to attend, then actual attendance and qualified follow-up |
| Webinar: warm or pipeline invitation pool | $20–60 | $600–1,800 | Usually a 7–21-day pre-event flight | Unsaturated eligible invitees, useful incremental registrations and correct registrant suppression |
| Website/engagement retargeting to proof or evaluation | $10–50 | $300–1,500 | 14–28 days if the pool is viable | Qualified actions and sufficient new eligible people; inspect repetition across all warm cells |
| Existing-pipeline support | $15–50 | $450–1,500 | 4–8 weeks for coverage and sales feedback; use the actual deal cycle for outcomes | Buying-committee coverage, sales-confirmed relevance and account progression rather than new-lead CPL |

The webinar figures in the monthly column are equivalents, not instructions to run for 30 days. For example, a cold flight at $100/day for 21 days costs $2,100; a distinct warm flight at $30/day for 14 days costs $420. Their planned combined spend is $2,520. Set the event cutoff in the account timezone and account for the event's local time. Do not leave invitation copy running after registration closes.

For a new account, begin with a small number of cells and a few distinct creatives within each funded cell. Two or three creative concepts can be a practical starting scope; check whether the budget can produce useful exposure for each. Multiple ads in one ad set are not a controlled equal-spend experiment. Stage additional markets, offers and formats when the first cells support a decision.

## ABM budgets by personalization level

These are alternative ways to organize media for the same funnel jobs above. **Do not add a full ABM allowance on top of the same accounts' education, ebook and retargeting budgets without counting their combined spend and frequency.** Define what the account, cluster or universe allowance already includes.

| ABM approach | Starting daily allowance | 30-day equivalent | Unit and evaluation window |
|---|---:|---:|---|
| 1:1 | $10–30 | $300–900 | Per individual account across the planned media cells. Review reach/committee coverage weekly; allow about 6–12 weeks for a sales-supported program, subject to the sales cycle. |
| 1:few | $30–100 | $900–3,000 | Per coherent account cluster, not per account. First message/delivery read in 3–4 weeks; account progression over roughly 6–12 weeks. |
| 1:many | $75–200 | $2,250–6,000 | Per funded segment of the approved named-account universe. First delivery/quality read in 3–4 weeks; commercial results over the actual buying cycle. |

Audience capacity and approved account value override these ranges. A tiny account may not support even the low end continuously. If an individual account cannot meet eligibility or absorb the platform minimum at useful frequency, propose a shorter affordable flight, an appropriate genuine 1:few cluster, or another sales/marketing channel. Preserve the intended named-account constraint. Do not add unrelated companies, countries or expansion just to make a 1:1 campaign spend.

The 1:1 allowance is not enough to run several ad sets below the platform minimum. Concentrate it into one viable media cell, phase the offers over time, or recost the plan. If three separately funded cells each require $10/day, their combined account allowance starts at $30/day, not $10/day. Dedicated research, bespoke creative, landing pages and sales activity need a separate resource budget; paid-media allocation alone does not implement 1:1 ABM.

## Optional format tests

An ad format is not an additional funnel stage. Fund a format test from its chosen job's budget or explicitly rebalance the total plan.

| Format test | Starting daily allowance | 30-day equivalent | Initial evaluation |
|---|---:|---:|---|
| Message or Conversation Ads | $30–100 | $900–3,000 | 14–28 days, or the relevant event flight. Verify available billing, delivery, response and qualified-action metrics. Small messaging audiences can exhaust reachable supply. |
| Text or Spotlight | $10–30 | $300–900 | 21–28 days. Check placement, actual clicks, destination quality and the intended contribution. |
| Article/newsletter promotion | $20–60 | $600–1,800 | 21–28 days. Measure relevant readership/engagement and the intended downstream action. |
| Follower growth, where strategically justified | $10–25 | $300–750 | 21–28 days. Inspect the relevance of acquired followers; do not present follower cost as a pipeline result. |

## Check the current platform controls

Checked 19 November 2026; recheck before launch, especially after hierarchy or currency changes.

- LinkedIn publishes a $10 minimum daily budget and a $100 starting lifetime minimum for inactive campaigns; duration affects the lifetime minimum. The live account validator is the final check for the selected configuration and currency. These minimums do not establish a useful test budget. [LinkedIn budget tips](https://business.linkedin.com/advertise/ads/best-practices/maximize-your-budget).
- A daily budget is an average. Current Help allows daily spend up to twice the configured daily amount. Continuous daily pacing is bounded over a Monday–Sunday week; use actual schedule controls and lifetime limits for a bounded flight. [Budget types and pacing](https://www.linkedin.com/help/lms/answer/a422101).
- Daily plus lifetime budgeting uses a continuous schedule in the documented configuration. A lifetime budget with a set schedule serves a different purpose. Dynamic Group Budget allocates at campaign level; ad sets do not also have their own budgets when it is enabled. [Budget hierarchy](https://www.linkedin.com/help/lms/answer/a422101).
- When a campaign total budget is set, new ad sets need sufficient unspent headroom: currently $100 USD, or the listed currency-specific amount, such as £80 GBP. This is a parent-budget launch requirement, not a recommended daily spend. [Minimum unspent budget](https://www.linkedin.com/help/lms/answer/a9486341).

Inspect account, parent and child schedules, holds and remaining caps together. A non-optimized campaign lifetime cap limits total spend; it does not automatically distribute the budget optimally between its ad sets. A bid is not a spend cap. Use the actual optimized action and billing basis when interpreting cost, especially for Sponsored Messaging.

## Recalculate for the new company

Collect the media ceiling/currency/period, ICP and markets, final eligible audience, existing warm pools, offers, expected auction costs, qualification definition, conversion rates, sales cycle, follow-up capacity and acceptable acquisition cost. Mark estimates and unknowns. Do not borrow another employer's CPM, CPL, win rate or budgets.

Use two calculations together:

```text
Exposure budget for the period
  = eligible members × assumed reach fraction × impressions per reached member
    ÷ 1,000 × assumed CPM

Outcome-led test budget
  = desired number of the defined outcomes × expected cost per that outcome

Planned daily pace
  = funded test budget ÷ scheduled delivery days
```

The exposure model is a planning approximation, not a delivery forecast or a proof of available inventory. Define frequency over a named period and count overlapping campaigns. For a small account with 800 eligible members, 60% assumed reach, eight impressions per reached member over 30 days and an assumed $80 CPM, modeled exposure costs $307.20. Those inputs are fictional assumptions. A $900 monthly allowance would imply a different reach/frequency assumption and should not be spent automatically.

For an ebook test seeking 20 raw leads at an assumed $120 CPL, the funded test is $2,400, or about $85.71/day over 28 days. Twenty is an illustrative planning input, not a statistically sufficient sample requirement. If only 20% become qualified held meetings, the expectation is four such meetings: useful directional learning, with substantial uncertainty. Review the matured cohort and sales feedback before scaling.

Where the model supports it, the feasible test must fit all three constraints: enough spend to answer the stated question, sufficient reachable supply, and the approved financial ceiling. If they conflict, revise the question, scope, allocation or duration rather than calling an underfunded test conclusive. Do not keep extending a failing test beyond its agreed ceiling.

## Anchor the budget in commercial economics

Define whether the target is **media-only CAC** or fully loaded acquisition cost. If it is fully loaded, reserve the non-media cost before deriving media allowances. Use matching cohorts, attribution scope and matured rates.

```text
Allowable media cost per qualified held meeting
  = allowable media CAC × qualified-held-meeting-to-customer rate

Allowable media cost per raw lead
  = allowable media cost per qualified held meeting × raw-lead-to-qualified-held-meeting rate
```

Fictional example: $3,000 allowable media CAC × 10% meeting-to-customer rate = $300 per qualified held meeting. At 20% lead-to-held-meeting conversion, the allowable raw-lead cost is $60. A campaign producing $120 raw leads would miss that modeled media target unless another assumption changes. Do not scale it simply because its platform CPL looks attractive. Validate rates and attribution; these equations do not prove incrementality or apply an acquisition CPL target to every awareness activity in isolation.

For top-of-funnel TLA/video, agree relevant reach/attention and a funded learning hypothesis while following aggregate qualified outcomes over time. For 1:1 ABM, budget against reachable committee coverage, account value and coordinated sales work. Keep sourced, influenced and incremental pipeline distinct.

## Example monthly portfolios

These are fictional USD allocations, not fixed funnel ratios. Reserve means unassigned money, not another active campaign.

| Portfolio | Funded cells | Planned media | Reserve | Total ceiling |
|---|---|---:|---:|---:|
| Focused $3,000 launch | Cold TLA/video $30/day; ebook $50/day; viable warm pool $10/day | $2,700 | $300 | $3,000 |
| $6,000 with a distinct ABM cluster | Cold prospecting $60/day; ebook $70/day; viable warm pool $20/day; 1:few cluster $30/day | $5,400 | $600 | $6,000 |

Both assume 30 scheduled days. If no warm pool exists, leave that allocation unassigned or deliberately reallocate it after checking test needs. In the second example, separate the cluster from broader acquisition or explicitly manage/account for overlap. These portfolios cannot promise enough conversions for a commercial decision; use the outcome calculation above. With a smaller ceiling, fund fewer viable cells, phase tests, or select another channel rather than launching every funnel and ABM tier at once.

For the real plan, total the actual flights and shared parent budgets once. Recheck the configured limits when dates, allocation or budget optimization changes. Budget headroom and a conservative pacing allowance matter when comparing intraday cash spend with average daily plans.

## Testing periods and decision rules

Set the hypothesis, comparison, primary metric, quality definition, expected lag, planned evidence, spend ceiling, review dates and stop rule before launch.

| Review | What to do |
|---|---|
| First 24–72 hours | Verify approval, delivery, tracking, targeting and routing. Fix technical failures or incorrect targeting immediately; a test window is not a reason to keep a broken setup running. |
| Days 7–14 | Diagnose delivery, relevant reach, spend pace, lead validity and audience pressure. Avoid declaring a commercial winner from a handful of immature outcomes. |
| End of the row's initial window | Decide whether to continue within the funded ceiling, revise one hypothesis, consolidate, stop or scale. State what the observed sample can support. |
| After the actual qualification/sales lag | Reconcile qualified meetings, opportunities and customers to their acquisition cohorts. Webinar evaluation includes attendance and post-event follow-up. |

For LinkedIn's native A/B test feature, the documented duration is two weeks to 90 days; that rule does not make every ordinary campaign a valid two-week experiment. Use the feature's current eligibility, comparison design and evidence guidance. [Native A/B test setup](https://www.linkedin.com/help/lms/answer/a1498125).

Routine creative variants do not ensure random assignment or equal exposure. Formal comparisons need a defined decision rule and sufficient evidence for the effect being tested. There is no universal lead count that makes every B2B test statistically reliable.

As an optional diagnostic, spending two to three times the expected cost of a primary response without one can trigger a technical/fit review once reporting lag has elapsed. This is not a platform rule or a statistical failure threshold. Do not apply it to a tiny 1:1 audience using expected opportunity cost, or to brand activity whose primary objective is reach. Stop at the agreed loss limit even when the result remains inconclusive.

## Scaling, holding and reducing spend

Before increasing a budget, verify that measurement works, the relevant cohort has matured enough, quality meets the agreed business criteria, follow-up capacity exists and eligible supply can absorb more exposure. Compare the same objective, audience temperature, period and attribution basis.

| Evidence | Proposed action |
|---|---|
| Acceptable quality/economics, healthy delivery and more qualified supply | Consider a modest increase within the approved total ceiling. |
| Relevant leads but too little matured evidence | Hold the allocation while the funded test continues; avoid treating noise as a winner. |
| Cheap leads with poor fit or weak meeting quality | Fix targeting, offer or handoff; do not scale on CPL alone. |
| High repetition with little new qualified reach | Reduce pressure, refresh content or replenish the qualified pool; do not solve saturation by raising the bid. |
| Low delivery because of a hold, date, permission, source or eligibility issue | Resolve the actual blocker before changing budget. |
| Broken primary measurement, wrong targeting, closed event or exhausted approved ceiling | Pause/propose a stop as authorized and resolve the cause. |

A practical starting change policy is **10–20% at one scheduled review, then observe roughly 5–7 days or the relevant reporting/qualification lag before another routine increase**. This is an operator heuristic to adapt to spend and data volume, not a LinkedIn requirement, a guaranteed safe learning threshold or permission to raise spend automatically. Event pacing may need a different pre-approved schedule. Fix urgent errors immediately.

Keep enough variables stable to interpret the change. Record the previous and new daily pace, actual remaining flight budget, parent cap, total simultaneous account spend, reason, evidence, review date and rollback. Several ad-set increases can breach the overall plan even when each increase looks small. Unspent reserve or an apparent winner does not expand the authorized ceiling. Material expansion into another geography, cluster, offer or format is a new test with its own budget and quality checks.

## Private budget-plan output

For each proposed funded cell record: purpose and ABM unit; objective/format; audience and eligible size; budget owner/control level; currency; daily pace; scheduled days; flight total; 30-day equivalent; parent limit; cost/reach/quality assumptions; evidence target; primary metric; cohort lag; stop/hold/scale condition; review date; and authorization state. Include the non-media resource budget, total concurrent spend, overlaps and reserve separately. Explain why each deferred cell is not yet funded.
