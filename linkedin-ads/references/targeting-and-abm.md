# Targeting and ABM

## Start with the new company's economics

Build the ICP from recent good-fit wins, retention/expansion, losses and sales capacity. Record company traits, problem, buying trigger, disqualifiers, deal value, margin, sales cycle and who buys. A job title is a way to reach the ICP, not the ICP itself. If evidence is thin, label the initial segment as a test hypothesis.

Choose which problem and product each segment is buying. Separate distinct products and buying groups when their messages, exclusions or commercial outcomes differ. Do not inherit a previous company's employee bands, industries, markets or recruitment-role exclusions.

Create a translation table:

| Business decision | Platform implementation | Check |
|---|---|---|
| Which companies can buy? | Named companies/company list, or industry and company-size filters | Correct entities, subsidiaries and sizes |
| Where can we sell? | Member location, usually permanent for residency-based plans | Member geography differs from company headquarters |
| Who owns the problem? | Relevant current job titles, or job function AND seniority | Local title variants and actual delivery demographics |
| Who else influences the deal? | Role-specific committee segments | Budget holder, technical evaluator and practitioners |
| Why now? | Verified account signals, CRM stage, offer and timing | Do not turn an unverified tech-stack label into a fact |
| Who should not receive this offer? | Customer/pipeline/converter/employee exclusions appropriate to this motion | Suppression freshness and scope |

## Choose a targeting method deliberately

**Native prospecting:** use company attributes and person attributes when the market can be described reliably and there is no complete account list. Avoid stacking every available filter. Test title targeting against a function-plus-seniority alternative in separate, understandable cells where useful.

**Company-list prospecting:** build a qualified account universe outside LinkedIn, match it, then narrow to the buying roles. The list's millions of member accounts are not millions of relevant decision-makers.

**Contact targeting:** reach matched people from an authorized first-party source. Additional filters can remove wanted contacts; do not mechanically reapply every firmographic rule to a prequalified attendee list.

**Behavioral retargeting:** identify the actual behavior, recency and source. A company whose employee engaged is different from the particular employee who engaged. Keep company-engagement audiences distinct from person-level retargeting.

**Predictive or automated prospecting:** use as a separate expansion test with appropriate seed quality. Determine whether supplied inputs are strict constraints or signals. Label expanded delivery honestly; do not call it strict ABM or assume a warm source makes every predicted member warm. [Official predictive audience guidance](https://www.linkedin.com/help/lms/answer/a1638426).

## Build Boolean logic before clicking

Example, fictional IT software company:

```text
Cold market = permanent target geography
  AND approved company list
  AND (security decision-maker titles OR approved equivalent titles)
  MINUS customers, open opportunities and internal/irrelevant employers

Warm market = target geography
  AND (eligible video viewers OR relevant document engagers OR relevant site visitors)
  AND buyer fit where needed
  MINUS customers, current offer submitters and pipeline if separately funded

Pipeline support = target geography
  AND open-opportunity companies
  AND relevant buying committee
  MINUS closed-won accounts unless this is an expansion motion
```

These are logical plans, not guarantees that all facet combinations are supported. Translate them to the current UI and verify its audience summary. Values inside a facet often combine as OR; use explicit narrowing for AND. Adding a company list to an OR group with broad industries can accidentally admit people outside the list. [LinkedIn audience building](https://www.linkedin.com/help/lms/answer/a420751).

Use this membership review when overlap matters:

| Situation | Intended consequence |
|---|---|
| Cold and warm eligibility overlap | Keep overlap intentionally for continuous education, or exclude warm from cold for a controlled sequential test |
| Strategic accounts also appear in a broad list | Give one cell priority and subtract it from the other; choose direction explicitly |
| Warm person works at an open-opportunity account | Route to pipeline support if distinct budgets/offers are intended |
| Ebook submitter | Exclude from the same acquisition offer; retain for relevant nurture |
| Webinar registrant | Remove from acquisition invites; retain for reminders and post-event paths |
| Customer | Exclude from new-logo spend; use a separate expansion campaign where appropriate |

Perfect exclusivity is not required for every program. Document the overlap that is intentional and monitor combined frequency.

## Design ABM around named accounts and sales coverage

ABM combines selected companies, differentiated reasons to care, sales coordination and account-level measurement. It can span every funnel stage. It is not merely a retargeting list or a small audience.

Define tiers for this company rather than treating tier names as universal:

| Motion | Investment | Good fit |
|---|---|---|
| One-to-one | Account-specific narrative, assets and sales actions | Few valuable accounts with active owner coverage and enough reachable members |
| One-to-few | Shared use case, industry, installed technology or trigger | Similar accounts where one useful story can serve the cluster |
| One-to-many | Approved list with common positioning and scalable content | Larger qualified account universe and limited personalization capacity |

Prioritize by evidenced fit, value, timing and ability to engage, not an arbitrary score. If a scoring model helps, show inputs/weights and let sales inspect it. Keep fit separate from engagement and stage: heavy engagement cannot repair fundamental poor fit.

Map champion, economic buyer, technical evaluator, procurement and influential users where relevant. Give each a distinct asset or reason to care. Company targeting can cover a committee without possessing everyone's email; it does not guarantee a named individual saw an ad.

For a tiny one-company cell, check final eligible members. If it cannot deliver, pool genuinely related accounts, include relevant committee functions, or use sales-owned contact paths. Do not pad CSVs, add unrelated companies, or widen geography solely to clear a minimum. Direct company selection is an alternative to bulk CSV upload for a small account set; the final audience must still qualify.

## Capacity and budget

Start with the fewest cells needed to answer the current decision. Multiple geographies, tiers, personas, funnel stages and formats multiply into many underfunded cells. Split only when it changes spend control, creative, eligibility, legal/operational requirements or learning.

Estimate reachable impressions as `eligible members × expected reach fraction × intended frequency`; estimate cost with a stated CPM range. These are planning assumptions, not a delivery promise. Reserve warm-audience spend in proportion to the available pool rather than forcing a standard funnel split. Use the new company's baseline and qualified outcomes to revise the plan.

## What transfers to another company

Transfer the decision process, evidence discipline, audience register, launch checks and learning cadence. Rebuild the company universe, buying roles, exclusions, offers, proof, economics, CRM lifecycle definitions and platform assets. Fresh audiences require fresh data and time to build; another employer's warm audiences are not portable company knowledge.
