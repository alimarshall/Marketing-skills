# Education video prep

Turns one buyer problem into a fully prepped long-form how-to video that teaches first and mentions the product only where it genuinely helps. The output is a prep doc the presenters and the videographer can work from.

## Inputs

- Company and product: `<fill in>`
- The one problem this video solves: `<fill in>`
- Who it's for (buyer job titles): `<fill in>`
- Presenters, their roles and what each one knows first-hand: `<fill in>`
- Buyer research and quote library (sales or customer calls): `<fill in>`
- Outside stats, each with source and year: `<fill in>`
- Product capability baseline and approved customer claims: `<fill in>`

If any input is missing, ask before drafting. Never invent a capability, number or quote.

## Structure of every video

| Part | Minutes | What it covers |
|---|---|---|
| Introductions | 0.5 | Each presenter says who they are and what the video will show. |
| 1. The problem | 2 | What the problem is and why it's getting worse. Stats plus buyer quotes. |
| 2. What teams do today | 2 to 3 | The common approaches, one or two lines each, with where each falls short. The product can appear here as one option among several. |
| 3. How to do it, step by step | 6 to 8 | Split by presenter: the business or process steps, then the technical or specialist steps, then launch and running it. |
| 4. What goes wrong | 3 to 4 | A table: problem, why it happens, the fix, who answers. |
| 5. First 90 days and wrap up | 1 to 2 | Month by month, what to keep doing after month 3, a short wrap-up, and the close. |

Record each part so it makes sense on its own, so clips can be cut later.

## Splitting the presenters

Give each person the parts they deal with every day. Whoever talks to buyers sets up the problem and the alternatives. Whoever implements or delivers the work leads the process steps, launch and what goes wrong. A technical or specialist presenter explains how it works on a whiteboard or simple diagram and gives the technical fixes. Write handover lines between them.

## What goes in each section

- **Talking points**, written as prompts in plain language so people say them in their own words. Only the introduction and close are written word for word.
- **A story prompt** for each presenter: a real moment from their own work that shows expertise, such as a problem they traced to its cause and how they fixed it, or what a customer's setup looked like before and after.
- **Stats and quotes** inline, with each quote kept exactly as said.
- **Product mentions where they fit**, kept short and specific:
  - as one option in "what teams do today";
  - after the technical steps, as the thing built to handle them ("This is why we built [product]...");
  - where the company's team helps with the hard part of the setup;
  - in the close, as an offer of ideas or feedback.

  Each one says what the product does, with approved proof where there is some. No hard sell.

## Close

A wrap-up that tells viewers one concrete thing to do this week, then a plain offer of help: "That's what we do at [company] with [product]. If you want ideas or feedback on anything to do with [topic], reach out."

## Prep and fact-check sections

- **Prep call questions** for each presenter, so they can confirm or correct the steps. When answers come back, fold them into the talking points and delete the answered questions.
- **Claims to check**: every stat with its source, every buyer quote marked as needing sign-off, any customer name marked as needing that customer's approval. Flag any claim broader than the approved version (for example "works with any system" when only named systems are approved).
- **Shoot plan**: booking (film one person's parts for several videos in one session), setup, a table of clips to cut for ads, and a before-filming checklist.

## Writing rules

- Plain, spoken language, the way the presenter would say it to a colleague.
- No "it's not X, it's Y" or "rather than" contrasts, no em dashes, no fake questions, no reflex lists of three, no uplift lines.
- Keep the prep doc clean: no "On screen" lines or "Talking points" labels unless asked.
- Buyer quotes stay anonymous (role and sector only) unless there's permission.

## When the user keeps a copy elsewhere

If the user maintains the script in another doc, show every change in chat word for word (where it goes, what it replaces, the new text) so they can paste it across.
