# Customer Proof to Ads

Make a verified result the argument. Preserve what was measured while finding a concrete buyer-relevant story around it.

## Establish the proof

Read the underlying source, not only the headline stat. Record exact wording, customer, source date/location, measurement period, population, denominator, metric definition, conditions and attribution status. Capture unknowns explicitly. Distinguish observed results, targets, estimates, predictions and anecdotal reactions.

Check the interpretation that changes the claim. Requests received outside working hours are not requests resolved outside working hours. Automated replies are not resolved cases. A reduction within one deployment is not a guarantee for all buyers. Do not round a number into a stronger promise or combine unrelated cohorts.

Company or customer attribution must match the available permission/context. Use approved branding when supplied and wanted. Otherwise a truthful unattributed proof direction may work; do not invent an endorsement or assume a logo is necessary. Publication clearance gaps can remain review notes while useful drafts proceed.

## Develop proof routes

Use the result to explore distinct angles rather than repeating the same statistic with different backgrounds:

- Stat first, followed by the buyer consequence and product's verified role.
- A specific moment that makes the result recognizable.
- A workflow or artifact demonstrating what changed.
- Capacity, experience or business outcomes within the measurement's scope.

A time-of-day story must specify the question or task and show what the offering actually did. An invented timestamp or fictional user is a constructed scenario, not a recorded customer event. Keep that distinction in the research record and avoid wording that presents the scenario as historical fact. Do not combine a real customer metric with a fabricated testimonial.

Show appropriate scope in the ad when omission would mislead. Keep qualification legible and concise; full source detail belongs in the evidence record or linked destination. Do not conceal a material limitation in a tiny footer.

## Write and direct

If requested, lead visually with the stat and use the supporting line to connect it to the buyer's problem. Prefer one strong result over a cluttered wall of proof. Avoid assuming that a percentage establishes speed, quality, availability and full resolution at once.

Choose real photography, a verified product sequence, typography or a concrete artifact to support the angle. A 24/7 availability claim requires its own support. For time-of-day ads, make the setting plausible without using staged imagery as evidence of a real event.

Return source-linked proof, claim-safe wording, angle, headline, supporting copy, caption and visual direction as requested. Label extrapolated directions as exploratory outside the ad. `buyer-research-to-ads` supplies buyer relevance; `brand-product-context` supplies product truth; `ad-creative-direction` and `ad-library-review` handle production and versions when available.
