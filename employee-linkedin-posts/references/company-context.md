# Company Context: Reference for Employee LinkedIn Posts

Fill this in once per company and keep it current. The skill reads it before every post. Anything left as `<fill in>` is something the skill will not guess at; it will ask instead.

Keep it short and factual. This file shapes the voice and the buyer's world; it is not a place for marketing copy. Every proof point and claim here should be one you would be happy to defend in public.

## Buyer field

`<fill in>`

The field your buyers work in and the practitioner identity your employees post from. One or two sentences. Examples: "Finance operations at mid-market companies", "Security and IT at regulated businesses", "HR and People Operations at scaling companies", "Engineering leadership at product companies".

## Mission

`<fill in>`

One sentence on why the company exists, in the buyer's terms. The skill uses this to keep posts pointed at the problem you care about, never quoted directly.

## Story in one line

`<fill in>`

The shortest version of what you do. If you have more than one product, how they fit together.

## Category narrative

`<fill in>`

Two or three sentences on the problem in the buyer's field that your category exists to fix. Write it as a practitioner would describe it, not as a tagline.

## Positioning (short)

`<fill in>`

One line per product: what it is and who it is for. Background for the 20% of product-adjacent posts; the skill will not paste this into posts.

## Product focus areas (intake Step 1)

`<fill in>`

The options an employee picks from in Step 1. Usually one per product (core product, AI or add-on product), plus "both". For each, one line on the problems it covers.

## Values

`<fill in>`

3 to 6 values, each with a one-line explanation of what it means in practice. The skill uses these to shape tone and substance and never states them in a post. Write the behaviour, not the slogan: "We don't make claims we can't prove" is more useful than "Integrity".

## Messaging pillars

`<fill in>`

If you have a messaging framework (see the positioning-brief skill), list each pillar with:
- its headline
- 2 to 4 supporting points
- the approved proof point for it, if any (see "Approved proof points" below)

Leave out anything not yet signed off.

## Buyer personas

`<fill in>`

2 to 4 personas. For each:
- **Role and titles** (for example Head of [function], VP [function], founder at early stage)
- **What they're drowning in** (the daily frustrations)
- **What they want** (outcomes, not features)
- **Where they research** (peer networks, communities, review sites, events)
- **Attitude to change and to AI** (curious, cautious, under pressure to show results)
- **Primary, secondary or tertiary audience**

Build these from real buyer research (call notes, interviews, research docs listed in `creative-strategy/references/inputs.md`), not assumptions.

## Lead messaging by persona

`<fill in>`

For each persona, the order in which the messaging pillars land: which one opens the door, which earns conviction, which supports, which closes. Plus one line of messaging note (for example "lead with operational relief, not vision" or "keep it simple, avoid enterprise language").

## When to lead with which product

`<fill in>`

Only needed if you have more than one product. A short list of buyer situations and which product (or problem) to lead with for each. Example shape: "Actively replacing their current system: lead with [core product]"; "Happy with their system but buried in repetitive requests: lead with [AI product]".

## Content lanes (intake Step 2)

`<fill in>`

5 to 8 recurring problem areas in the buyer's field that employees can credibly post about. Name them in the buyer's language. Examples from different fields:
- Finance: month-end close, spend control, forecasting, audit prep
- Security: access reviews, audit readiness, vendor risk, incident response
- HR or People Ops: onboarding, people data and reporting, scaling the team, compliance across countries

The skill adds "AI in [buyer's field]", "General [buyer field] practice", "Other" and "Choose for me" automatically.

## Key products or capabilities by persona

`<fill in>`

For each persona, the 3 to 5 capabilities that matter most to them. Use your capability baseline as the source; do not list anything that is not shipped. Background only; posts lead with the problem, not the capability.

## AI product summary

`<fill in>`

Only if you have an AI product or AI features. What it does in plain verbs, what it does not do, and any security or compliance facts that are verified and public. The skill uses only what is written here for the AI lane.

## Approved proof points

`<fill in>`

Numbers, outcomes and customer quotes cleared for public use, each with:
- the claim, word for word
- the source (case study, survey, report) and date
- whether the customer has consented to being quoted or named

If something is not on this list, the skill will not use it. Run claim-verification before adding anything.

## Buying triggers

`<fill in>`

The moments that make a buyer start looking: growth thresholds, a new leader joining, a failed request from the board, an error or compliance near-miss, expansion into new markets, frustration with an incumbent after a contract period, a merger or separation. Write them for your field. The skill uses these to pick timely topics when an employee says "choose for me".

## Language rules

`<fill in>`

- **Words and phrases to avoid** (house banned list, and any category terms you never use, for example "chatbot" if your AI product does more than chat)
- **Preferred terms** (how you name the buyer, the category, the product's actions)
- **Spelling convention** (British or American)
- **Competitor mentions** (normally: never by name in employee posts)

If you have house register rules, point to them here and run copy-register-check on drafts.
