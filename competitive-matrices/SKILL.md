# Competitive matrices and the differentiation reference

Placeholders: **[Product]** (one row or column per product line), **[Competitors]**, **[Repo]**, **[Wiki]**.

## Works with

- **Takes in:** product docs and release notes, competitor-dossiers, buyer research and the VOC library.
- **Produces:** the capability baseline, master capability matrix, communication matrix, differentiation reference (approved claims) and verification tracker.
- **Next:** pillar-landing-pages and use-case-ad-variations (approved claims and lead features), abm-account-multithread and customer-proof-from-usage-reports (the baseline as a claims check). claim-propagation-sweep keeps it current when the product changes.

## 1. Capability baseline (build first)
The truth about your own product, one doc per feature area: what it does today, what it does not do, what's on the roadmap (never sold as shipped), known bugs and limits, and a verdict against current claims. Sources: product docs, release channels, the product itself, the product team. The product owner's correction beats any doc. Everything else is built on this.

## 2. Master capability matrix
- One table per product line (e.g. core product vs its competitors; an AI add-on vs its competitors).
- Rows are capabilities. Columns are [Product] and each competitor.
- Ratings: **Leader** (clear advantage), **Parity** (table stakes), **Trails** (they win), **Building** (in progress, sell the direction not the capability), **N/A** (doesn't compete there).
- Cells are short: the rating plus a few words of reason. No quotes; quotes live in dossiers and buyer research.
- Rating rules: base it on evidence, not marketing. Sales intel beats vendor docs. When a competitor ships, re-rate both their cell and yours: a lead on "has X" becomes Parity once they have X; your lead may move to maturity, price or depth.
- Keep a short version line at the top; no dated notes in cells.

## 3. Communication matrix
Per feature: description (what it really does), buyer pain (in buyers' words), outcome, proof (quote or metric), confidence, tier (**Lead** / **Supporting** / **Table stakes**), where to use it (homepage, feature page, sales deck, ads), competitive angle.
- Few things are Lead: differentiated against the real alternatives, evidenced in buyer research and provable.
- A feature with no buyer pain in the research does not lead.
- Mark anything not yet safe for external copy.

## 4. Differentiation reference
- Numbered claims. Each: the claim, why it matters, proof (quotes, metrics, case studies), register variants (SMB, enterprise, technical), caveats.
- Never renumber; retire a claim by marking it retired.
- "Where we trail" section: honest gaps, each with the evidence and what to say.
- A sales-talk-points table: which claim wins which deal type.

## 5. Verification tracker
Spreadsheet (ideally generated by a script so it can be rebuilt): ID, open question or unconfirmed claim, area, where it appears, why it matters, priority, status (Open / Resolved / Partly resolved), notes, date. Every claim that isn't confirmed goes here before it goes in copy.

## Maintenance
- **Competitor change:** update the competitor's dossier, then their matrix cell, the related claim, the communication-matrix angle. Re-rate your own cell if your lead changed.
- **Your product ships something** ("we shipped X"): read the release note fully (what's new, what it doesn't do, temporary numbers). Update the capability baseline, then grep every matrix, claim, dossier summary table and comparison for the old state ("pending", "not live", "no X", the feature name) and fix each. Close or narrow the matching gap and tracker row honestly.
- **VOC cross-check:** compare quotes about your own product in the VOC library against the differentiation reference and communication matrix. Add missing proof to the matching claim. Flag claims with lots of fresh proof (candidates for promotion) and repeated loss reasons that no claim answers.
- **Confirmed decisions** (e.g. an implementation timeline) get written everywhere at once and the tracker row is resolved.
- Mirror every edit to the wiki in place. Log runs in the sweep log only.

## Writing
Short plain sentences, no em dashes, no "not just X, Y" constructions. Never claim roadmap as shipped. Never invent a number, quote or competitor capability.

## Shared conventions

- Find inputs in the workspace first: the files other skills in this set produce (capability baseline, buyer research, VOC library, evidence docs, customer proof files, dossiers, matrices, approved claims). Ask only for what's missing. Never fill a gap with assumptions about the company.
- Never invent a capability, stat, quote or competitor fact. Quotes are verbatim; named customer material stays internal until the customer signs off.
- Claim tags are SAFE, NEEDS VERIFY: (what settles it) or INTERNAL ONLY. Keep the tags you receive and pass them on.
- The user's own writing rules and brand voice override the defaults in this skill.
