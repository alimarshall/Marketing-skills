# Ad Creative Direction

Make the visual express the angle before adding decoration. Use the current company's identity, placement and product truth. Do not substitute a familiar campaign's style for the brief.

## Choose the visual argument

Read the approved angle, buyer, supporting copy, claim limits, brand references and placement. Identify what the viewer should understand first and what the image must prove or make felt. Choose a treatment based on that job:

- Photography for a recognizable human moment, ambition or environment.
- Product or service experience for a concrete capability or change.
- Type and proof for a meaningful verified result.
- Diagram or technical drawing for a real relationship, system or handoff.

Mixed treatments should add meaning. Do not put UI on every photograph or force every concept into a card. Match the requested number of concepts and final assets; do not expand a brief into an unsolicited batch.

## Compose for the placement

Establish headline, supporting line, focal subject and product/proof hierarchy. Reserve space for platform overlays and the requested CTA policy. Use a mobile-size preview to assess reading, not only the full-resolution canvas. Favor fewer readable elements over shrinking type.

For photo plus UI, protect eyes, faces and the gesture that explains the scene. Keep floating panels level unless the brand and concept call for perspective. Use enough contrast for text; transparency is a treatment, not a requirement. A subtle subject mask can tuck UI behind a person when it remains readable and makes spatial sense. Avoid clipping important words or distorting hands and hair.

Use actual product assets and `product-ui-storytelling` when showing operational steps. Decorative glass, nested containers and technical lines must not obscure the product story. Use authorized logos within their real context; do not scatter integration marks as generic proof.

## Produce only the requested output

A brief can end with composition, image direction, copy placement and asset requirements. For artwork, use the available image-generation or design skill required by the chosen tool. Use photography or approved stock when requested; do not pass generated imagery off as documentary photography. Check stock usage terms when sourcing, and preserve provenance. Evaluate natural anatomy, lighting, scale, reflections and scene logic; regenerate or replace flawed imagery instead of hiding it with copy.

Render the final placement and inspect at delivery and mobile size. Check text, logos, contrast, edge crops and unintended UI artifacts. A generated preview is not evidence of actual product behavior.

## Handoff

Return concept ID, angle, visual rationale, copy, placement, image provenance, editable source and exports when produced. State what is concept UI versus sourced UI in review notes. With multiple versions, use `ad-library-review` to maintain the review set. Use `ads-workflow` for broader coordination if available. Preserve accepted choices and apply feedback across every active export that uses the rejected element.
