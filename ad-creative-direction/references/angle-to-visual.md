# Match the creative to the angle

Read when turning an approved argument into a visual or deciding whether an existing asset fits. The visual should make the argument easier to understand or feel. A beautiful image that adds a different story can weaken the ad.

## Write the visual job before choosing the image

Carry forward the avatar, awareness state, buyer moment, tension, supported product job and intended outcome. Finish this sentence in internal notes:

> Because this ad is about [specific tension or opportunity], show [moment, artifact or relationship] so the viewer understands [meaning].

Choose what the picture must communicate and what the headline, supporting line and product evidence will explain. A photograph does not have to explain the entire product. The complete asset should make the audience and offering clear without requiring the paid caption to rescue it.

## Choose a treatment that carries the argument

| Angle | Useful visual argument | Common mismatch |
|---|---|---|
| Repetitive work or chasing | The recurring artifact, request or handoff; a supported completed sequence | An unrelated stressed person with no clue what is stuck |
| Instant help in a specific moment | A plausible setting and the actual question answered or task progressed | A night scene used to imply round-the-clock resolution without evidence |
| Leadership, ambition or scale | A person making decisions, a credible working environment or wider team context | A tiny administrative task that shrinks the larger promise |
| Visibility and decision confidence | A real question, useful report or clear before/after in what can be seen | Random charts, glowing networks or a generic futuristic office |
| Trust and control | The actual review, approval or access boundary relevant to the concern | Decorative locks, circuit lines and unexplained badges |
| Customer proof | A prominent verified result and its buyer consequence | A testimonial-looking portrait of someone unrelated to the result |
| Physical product or local service | The real object in use or a recognizable service moment | Software UI added to make the offering appear more sophisticated |

These are options, not mandatory formats. A typography-led ad can express ambition; a photo-only ad can express relief. Choose based on the brief and what the viewer needs to recognize.

## Test the fit

- **Story:** Does the visible action match the promised change? Check setting, props, expressions and product state.
- **Specificity:** Could this exact composition accompany any unrelated headline? If so, add a relevant moment or artifact, or select a different treatment.
- **Scope:** Does the creative show the advertised breadth? A platform-scale promise needs broader context than one unexplained notification; a narrow use case needs a concrete experience.
- **Evidence:** Does the image imply an unsupported claim, customer endorsement, integration or completed action?
- **Attention:** Is the intended first read still first at mobile size? Remove elements that compete with it.

Do not force a product overlay to fix a weak photo. Change the scene or the visual route. Conversely, do not remove useful product evidence solely to keep the image clean.

## Make variants test something

Distinguish an angle test from an execution test. A new argument needs different evidence or stakes; a different crop or person is an execution variant. When comparing visual treatments for the same angle, keep the promise and proof stable where practical and record what changed.

Return a visual rationale with each concept: what the viewer should recognize, why this treatment fits, what the product/proof contributes and what is deliberately left to copy. Feed the chosen direction into the production brief and keep its concept ID through review.
