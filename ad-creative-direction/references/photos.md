# Photos: source, create and use

Read when a concept needs people, workplaces, homes, products or other photographic imagery. Follow the company's preferences and the selected angle. Natural realism is an art direction, not a claim that an image records a real customer event.

## Choose the source

| Source | Useful when | Keep track of |
|---|---|---|
| Approved company or customer photography | Identity, real environment or authentic product detail matters | Approved uses, attribution and any restrictions on edits |
| Licensed stock | A believable everyday situation is needed quickly | Original asset URL/ID, licence for the planned advertising use and relevant release information |
| Commissioned shoot | Specific gestures, environments or consistent campaign casting are hard to find | Shot list, usage/release arrangements and required placement crops |
| Generated scene or approved image edit | The brief permits it and a specific composition or scenario is useful | Generation/edit provenance, source references and acceptance status |

Image search is discovery, not a licence. Do not use an arbitrary search thumbnail as production stock. Prefer documented commercial-use sources and verify the terms for the intended use, including sensitive-use restrictions when relevant. Do not attribute a real customer's result to a stock or generated person.

## Brief the photograph before searching or generating

Describe a moment, not just a demographic: who is doing what, where, at what time, and why that moment supports the angle. Specify believable props, wardrobe and the emotional register. Avoid treating an age, gender or office style as proof of a job title.

Plan subject position, gaze, hands, negative space, headline area and any product overlay. Name the actual delivery ratios and keep enough environment for alternate crops. For a portrait, decide whether the face, hands or surrounding context is the essential detail. Do not hope to repair an unsuitable crop by covering it with UI.

For stock, search by situation, action and composition as well as subject. Evaluate full-resolution candidates in the planned crop. For a shoot, turn those requirements into a shot list with room for copy and a few useful framing alternatives.

## Generate or edit when appropriate

Use the available image-generation skill/tool and its instructions. Inspect supplied references before editing them. Preserve accepted photo direction and identity when the brief calls for it; do not replace an approved image merely to make a fresh variation.

A useful prompt structure is:

> Photographic scene of [specific action] in [credible environment], with [natural light and mood]. [Subject position and camera framing]. Leave [area] usable for the planned headline or UI. Preserve [important face, hands, prop or contextual detail]. Deliver for [ratio].

Add only the camera or material details that help the result. Repeatedly asking for “cinematic, premium, ultra-realistic” does not substitute for a plausible scene. Prefer ordinary details, restrained expressions, natural skin texture and consistent lighting over glossy, generic perfection. Generate the scene separately from final typography, logos and operational product UI; composite those from the correct assets as real layers.

Do not put private records into image prompts unnecessarily. Fictional people, times and scenarios must not be described as documented customer events. Keep provenance in the review record; apply any required public disclosure for the actual use.

## Integrate the photo into the ad

Let the photo carry the human moment and the product carry the action. If the angle works with photography alone, keep it that way. When UI adds meaning, use the actual interface and a short supported sequence. Match the depicted viewpoint and channel.

Reserve faces, eyes and the important gesture. Keep UI large enough to read on mobile and use controlled contrast rather than excessive transparency. Subject masking can place a small part of the photo in front of a panel when it looks natural; never hide essential text or leave cutout halos. Preserve editable type and separate photo, UI, mask and overlay layers when the tools allow.

## Review and reuse

Inspect anatomy, hand-object contact, reflections, screen direction, shadows, depth, wardrobe and scene logic. Also check crop, readability and consistency with the rest of the campaign. Use a different image when the scene is fundamentally wrong; do not hide defects behind copy.

Maintain an asset ID, source/licence or generation record, permitted uses, crops, active concept IDs and accepted/rejected status. Vary people and environments deliberately without abandoning the campaign's visual register. Avoid repeating one face across unrelated angles by default. When an image is rejected, replace its active uses through the review library and retain clearly labelled historical versions only where useful.
